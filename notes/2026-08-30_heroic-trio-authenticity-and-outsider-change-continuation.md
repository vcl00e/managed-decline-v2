# Narrative-quality audit of the heroic-trio proposal

The proposal has become **substantially stronger**, but I would not yet treat it as a complete top-tier narrative design.

What we now have is an unusually good **premise, character-authenticity framework and causal theory for how the heroes might genuinely change Britain**. What is still missing is much of the machinery that turns those things into a story people become emotionally obsessed with: the player's personal dramatic position, trio chemistry, memorable antagonists and supporting characters, scene-level conflict, romantic pleasure, pacing, dialogue execution, and an emotionally satisfying ending.

So PR #16 is fine as a **research/design record**. I would not yet interpret every proposal inside it as settled narrative design.

---

# 1. The premise is now genuinely strong

> **Three impossible heroes set out to save Britain. The country laughs—until they start winning.**

I think this is the strongest headline premise we've found for *Managed Decline*.

It contains contradiction, aspiration, comedy and forward movement immediately. It also dramatically improves the game's legibility from the outside. Someone can understand what the game is *about* without understanding the simulation architecture.

The current proposal also correctly refuses to make their ambition the joke. Their methods, personalities, disagreements and the world around them can be funny while their desire to change things remains sincere.

**Keep this.**

But one distinction needs to become explicit:

> **The trio are the headline/public protagonists. The player is the experiential protagonist.**

That distinction solves a great deal.

The world may remember Lord Pothole, Tabitha and Gary as the people who attempted to save Britain. But the actual experience belongs to **the player's life**.

---

# 2. The largest problem is currently the player

The proposal says the player can join, romance, sabotage or ignore the heroes. That's good.

But that's not sufficient.

The current danger is:

> Interesting heroes have an interesting adventure → occasionally invite player → player chooses relationship to their adventure.

That is still fundamentally **content being delivered to the player**.

Earlier accepted work reached a stronger conclusion:

> A scenario is usually most compelling when it collides with **something the player was already trying to do**.

The hall became dramatically stronger when the player needed the hall for *their own plan*, rather than when Maya simply informed them that the hall was threatened.

That principle needs to survive the new epic storyline.

### Example

Weak:

> Tabitha asks whether you want to come to tomorrow's protest.

Stronger:

> You're trying to get someone to hire you.  
> The person interviewing you is involved in the dispute.  
> Tabitha is leading tomorrow's protest against them.  
> You're also sleeping with Tabitha.

Now there isn't a "Tabitha quest."

There is **your life colliding with Tabitha's historical moment**.

That needs to become a central requirement.

The heroic campaign should have **ports into the player's existing desires**:

| Player wants... | Campaign collides through... |
|---|---|
| romance | hero relationship |
| career | employer/institution |
| money | economic consequence |
| status | public association |
| friendship | competing loyalties |
| housing | campaign issue |
| creative project | venue/public controversy |
| quiet life | disruption caused by movement |
| political involvement | direct participation |
| nothing to do with it | friends/work/place affected anyway |

This preserves the accepted core fantasy:

> **Build your life → something intersects it → decide what matters → act → live with what happened.**

Without this correction, the trio could accidentally turn the player into a fourth-wheel spectator.

---

# 3. The three heroes are much better defined — but still too functional

The authenticity dossiers are one of the strongest parts of the proposal.

Lord Pothole is no longer "the funny one."

Tabitha is no longer "the human one."

Gary is no longer "the economics one."

The source-locked invariants, contradictions and false-friend rules are exactly the right way to preserve parody while inventing a substantial original person.

But the final coalition table partially falls back into RPG-class thinking:

> Pothole = framing  
> Tabitha = mobilisation  
> Gary = diagnosis

These should be **signature tendencies, not monopolies**.

Otherwise they'll become mechanically predictable.

Sometimes Tabitha should spot the structural problem first.

Sometimes Gary should be astonishingly good at getting ordinary people to come with him.

Sometimes Pothole should quietly perform the boring administrative work nobody else bothered to do.

Their specialties should affect what they instinctively attempt—not define the only thing they're capable of doing.

That matters particularly for Tabitha. There is a subtle danger of making the two men responsible for "analysis" and "public symbolism" while the young woman becomes "the one who's good with people." The current dossier explicitly warns against reducing her to the trio's human heart; the actual narrative needs to honour that warning.

---

# 4. Their arcs are currently too neat

This is one area I would actively change.

Current formulations such as:

> Pothole → durable civic competence

> Tabitha → accepts responsibility for who belongs

> Gary → accepts distributed expertise and succession

are good **pressures**, but they're too clean as predetermined destinations.

They risk becoming:

> here is the flaw → here is the lesson → character learns lesson.

That's not the subtle character development previously established for *Managed Decline*.

More importantly, Tabitha's current formulation gets close to pre-answering her ideological story for her. If her arc is secretly:

> nationalist character eventually learns to become more inclusive,

then the player will feel the author behind her.

Gary has the equivalent danger:

> passionate simplifier learns to listen to sensible experts.

And Pothole:

> eccentric outsider learns responsible government.

All three are too conventional.

### Better formulation

Give each character an **arc question**, not a required lesson.

| Hero | Dramatic pressure |
|---|---|
| Pothole | What happens when the joke acquires genuine responsibility? |
| Tabitha | What happens when the symbol gains enough power that other people's lives depend on what it means? |
| Gary | What happens when explaining the system is no longer the same thing as changing it? |

Different playthroughs can produce different answers.

They may grow.

They may become worse.

They may compromise.

They may discover a version of themselves neither the player nor they expected.

They may succeed without "fixing" the trait causing the tension.

That would be much more human.

---

# 5. We have not yet designed why the trio love each other

This is probably the **single biggest character gap**.

We understand why their political abilities are complementary.

We don't yet understand why, after sixteen awful hours together in a wet British town, **they actually want to see one another again tomorrow**.

If these are the headline heroes, this matters enormously.

The eventual fracture means nothing unless players first become attached to the group itself.

Each pair needs its own relationship:

| Pair | Must eventually establish |
|---|---|
| Pothole ↔ Tabitha | something they admire; something infuriating; some private comic language |
| Pothole ↔ Gary | a genuine dependency beyond political utility; disagreement that isn't merely intellectual |
| Tabitha ↔ Gary | affection or respect strong enough that ideological conflict actually hurts |
| All three | rituals, shared history, remembered disasters, stupid jokes, unspoken loyalties |

And sometimes one needs to defend another **when doing so has no strategic value**.

That's where a political coalition becomes a heroic party.

We need scenes of them missing trains, sharing food, getting soaked, covering for one another, arguing over sleeping arrangements, celebrating something nobody else understands, turning up when one of them genuinely needs help, being bored together, and laughing at an event that went catastrophically wrong.

Those aren't filler.

They are what makes the later national stakes emotionally meaningful.

---

# 6. The proposal badly needs memorable antagonists

At present, the opposition is mostly:

> institutions, parties, donors, hostile movements, media, structures.

That's realistic.

It is insufficient dramatically.

A story of this scale needs recurring **people who can push back**.

Not a cartoon prime minister as the final boss. Not an Evil Corporation CEO.

But people with intelligence, their own constituencies, good arguments, something personally at stake, the ability to adapt to the heroes, and occasional genuine moral superiority over them.

There should be people who defeat Gary because he has misunderstood implementation.

People who expose Tabitha's weakest instincts accurately.

People who discover how to turn Lord Pothole's own performance against him.

People whom the player may sincerely prefer.

And crucially:

> **Some opponents should later become allies, and some allies should become opponents.**

The existing hidden-situation design already rejects a world where cynicism always turns out to be correct: apparent corruption may be incompetence, mocked programmes can sometimes work, and institutions can sometimes be right.

That principle becomes even more important now.

If every institution exists merely to demonstrate why Gary/Tabitha/Pothole are right, the satire dies.

---

# 7. Ordinary people must not become campaign resources

This is another significant risk introduced by the political research language.

The research usefully talks about witnesses, constituency, organisers, legal standing, allies, journalists and insiders.

Good internally.

But if the storytelling starts seeing people that way, *Managed Decline* will quietly change its thesis.

Earlier work identified exactly this danger:

> If every relationship becomes leverage, the game's systems are saying **people are tools**.

Someone whose hospital is threatened isn't principally:

> `+1 WITNESS`.

They are a person who may want the heroes' help, despise the heroes, accept the practical help but reject the movement, use Tabitha's audience, think Gary has completely misunderstood them, find Lord Pothole incredibly annoying, or not want their private tragedy made symbolic at all.

Sometimes the ethically correct thing for the heroes to do may be to **leave someone's story small**.

That would be powerful.

---

# 8. There is a better deep theme hiding underneath the political theme

The current umbrella political question is strong:

> **Who owns Britain, who is Britain for, and who gets to decide?**

Keep it.

But all three heroes have another extraordinary thing in common.

They are all, in different ways, **public representations**.

Lord Pothole is literally a persona.

Tabitha is a person transformed into an institutional representation and then an internet symbol.

Gary's political authority is inseparable from his carefully repeated autobiographical public story.

That creates a second, much more personal thematic spine:

> **Can you turn yourself into a symbol powerful enough to change reality without the symbol consuming the person?**

That theme connects politics, celebrity, satire, social media, romance, authenticity, public/private identity, burnout and parody itself.

And it immediately makes the romances more relevant.

The lover gets to know someone whom everyone else encounters as an image.

The campaign needs the image.

The person sometimes wants to escape it.

Now the private story and national story are about the **same underlying problem**.

That could be one of the strongest thematic ideas in the entire game.

---

# 9. The macro structure is good, but it is too visibly linear

The current architecture—

> impossible coalition → pattern → organisation → leverage victory → capture → fracture → political window → breakthrough → durability

—is excellent as a **writer's causal model**.

I would not make it the actual chapter sequence too rigidly.

Otherwise the audience will feel the machinery:

> Ah, we're at the movement-building act now. Next comes establishment backlash.

Use the accepted landmark/connective-content model instead.

Some events should be iconic landmarks with strong authored identity. The circumstances, relationships and meaning vary. Connective life between them can be much freer.

I would probably preserve a few structural landmarks:

**formation/convergence → first undeniable victory → severe strategic crisis → national opportunity → breakthrough/defeat → human aftermath**

Everything between those can have substantial variation.

Even the "fracture" shouldn't necessarily mean:

> the party must break up in every run.

What should be unavoidable is that their incompatible methods are subjected to a **serious stress test**.

Whether that produces a split, compromise, strange coalition or complete disaster can depend on history.

---

# 10. Political opportunity must never feel like deus ex machina

The historical mechanism is excellent.

It gives the heroes a believable chance.

But narratively it creates a new danger:

> twenty hours of buildup → conveniently timed national scandal opens the door.

That will feel authored in the bad sense unless the opportunity has been **anticipated**.

The court case should have existed.

The election should have been approaching.

The governing party's fracture should have been visible.

The document should have a history.

The vulnerable institution should already be under pressure.

Then the unexpected event changes the meaning of things the player already knows.

This is exactly what the existing setup/payoff architecture is designed to do: professional-feeling stories create the pleasure of recognising **"that earlier thing mattered."**

The player should experience the great opening as:

> **Oh shit. This is what all that was for.**

Not:

> A random crisis has spawned the finale.

---

# 11. The historical theory should stay almost completely invisible

The weakest possible implementation would expose:

> framing  
> organisation  
> leverage  
> executable remedy  
> political opportunity  
> durability

as campaign mechanics.

That would recreate precisely the system-forward failure already observed.

In the comparative prototype, explicit goals and exposed optimisation made things clearer but drastically less immersive; the dialogue baseline was preferred because the player enjoyed interpreting the situation and implicitly deciding what mattered.

So the twelve-stage political-change chain should be:

> **a writer's plausibility test**

not:

> **the player's campaign loop UI.**

The player experiences a lawyer calling, a volunteer cancelling, a newspaper changing tone, a councillor unexpectedly offering a meeting, Gary desperately needing a document, Tabitha realising half the people she expected haven't turned up, or Pothole suddenly being asked whether he would actually stand.

The system can know why those things constitute campaign capacity.

The player should experience **life**.

---

# 12. Local stories must not become formulaic campaign nodes

The six-residue idea is useful:

material outcome, evidence, constituency, public meaning, institutional response, relationship consequence.

But it should be a **possibility set**, not a requirement that every substantial scenario produces all six.

Otherwise every episode becomes:

> local problem → find evidence → recruit people → press coverage → relationship consequence.

Players will learn the formula rapidly.

Some episodes should matter nationally.

Some should matter only to Tabitha.

Some should repair something between Pothole and Gary.

Some should be a disastrous night out that later supplies one accidental contact.

Some should lead nowhere politically.

Some should look politically irrelevant and return twenty hours later.

Some should be genuinely **private**.

The accepted narrative architecture already distinguishes landmarks from connective tissue and warns against making everything equally important.

---

# 13. We need more "Britain worth saving"

This becomes substantially more important now that the heroes explicitly claim they want to rescue it.

They cannot spend forty hours moving between failing institutions.

The existing design has already reached the better thematic position:

> **This place is wonderful. Why on earth are we letting parts of it become like this?**

The game should contain genuinely beautiful, funny and pleasurable Britain: parks, pubs, coast, old buildings, ridiculous traditions, clubs, countryside, music, friends' houses, local rituals, wealthy spaces, public institutions that work, strangers being unexpectedly decent.

And each hero should love something concrete.

Not speeches about Britain.

Something we actually **see them love**.

Without that, "save Britain" is a slogan.

With it, the national ambition becomes emotional.

---

# 14. The heroes need victories that are simply allowed to be awesome

The dramatic tableaux are an excellent idea.

But I would revise one detail from the original proposal.

We repeatedly suggested:

> heroic pose → immediately show wet coats, awkward logistics and deflation.

That contrast is often excellent.

It should **not become mandatory**.

Otherwise we accidentally tell the audience:

> don't worry, we're going to puncture every sincere heroic moment.

That contradicts the corrected premise.

Sometimes Lord Pothole should stand in the ridiculous setting and be **genuinely magnificent**.

Sometimes Tabitha should accomplish something that makes the player think:

> Bloody hell.

Sometimes Gary should walk into a room where nobody thought he had a chance and absolutely destroy the argument.

Then let the scene breathe.

Satire does not require refusing catharsis.

---

# 15. The romances are promising but currently too political

The integration principle is right:

> romance changes the adventure rather than sitting in a separate dating mode.

Keep it.

But the current descriptions lean heavily toward strategy, trust, public legitimacy, scandal and movement consequences.

The relationships also need lust, playfulness, boring mornings, private jokes, affection, physical comfort, jealousy unrelated to politics, arguments about trivial things, holidays, meeting friends, sleeping badly, wanting incompatible lives and doing absolutely nothing useful together.

Otherwise the player is dating **the campaign role**, not the character.

And romance should never become:

> **the route through which the player fixes that hero's ideological flaw.**

That would be especially damaging to Tabitha.

---

# 16. The civilian route is a great idea but currently not good enough

"Don't join; see the campaign on the television in the pub" is a useful example.

It isn't a route.

If we're genuinely promising:

> you can just live your life,

then that life needs its own strong desires, relationships, embarrassments, achievements and history.

The correct implementation is not a second bespoke campaign.

It's one shared world.

The heroes' movement intersects differently with work, rent, transport, dating, family, neighbourhood, media, friends and institutions.

The campaign becomes one enormous evolving situation inside the hidden situation network. The player can be deeply embedded, loosely connected or almost completely external.

That architecture already supports one hidden reality being experienced through radically different partial perspectives.

That is how "ordinary civilian" becomes a genuine playthrough rather than deliberately missing the good story.

---

# 17. The pacing currently risks becoming exhausting

The national campaign naturally wants to escalate.

That is dangerous.

*Managed Decline* already has a much better pacing architecture: spacious → focused → crowded → recovering, with aftermath and ordinary life after intense scenes.

The heroic story needs to obey that.

A huge win may be followed by work on Monday, a date, someone's birthday, two fairly uneventful weeks, a small domestic problem, the heroes not speaking for a while, or an entirely unrelated attractive opportunity.

This doesn't weaken momentum.

It creates contrast.

Routine was already accepted specifically because normality makes change emotionally visible.

Without normal life, "historical event" stops feeling historical because everything is historical.

---

# 18. Dialogue and first-pass comprehension are currently major risks

The heroic proposal is intellectually dense.

Politics.

Policy.

Institutions.

Coalition strategy.

Parody references.

History.

Relationships.

This could easily produce dreadful scenes where three clever characters explain competing theories for eight minutes.

V008 already supplied an extremely clear warning: a scene containing too many concepts required a second playthrough simply to understand what was happening, and the resulting experience was neither fun nor interesting. The accepted correction was that **basic scene comprehension must work on the first normal playthrough; only subtext should reward replay.**

So the heroic campaign needs a strict rule:

> **Ideological complexity belongs across the story. Scene objectives should remain simple enough to understand immediately.**

A scene can be:

> Are we endorsing this candidate?

It should not simultaneously require the player to understand:

> electoral coordination + tax strategy + migration framing + media capture + coalition legitimacy + Gary's childhood.

The scene contract already gives the correct test: why now, what does everyone want, what does the player know, what is the live question, what leverage does the player have, what turns, why does it end, what remains?

Use that aggressively.

---

# 19. Gary must not steal the player's job of understanding the world

A subtle problem in the current story outline is:

> local stories happen → Gary begins connecting them.

Sometimes, yes.

But one of *Managed Decline's* best intended player skills is **reading society**: seeing several partial manifestations and recognising they connect before anybody explicitly tells you.

If Gary always explains the causal pattern, he becomes a walking solution manual.

Much better:

Gary sees one connection.

Tabitha sees another.

Pothole notices a repeated institutional phrase.

The player may have seen something none of them has.

Someone may be completely wrong.

Then the conversation becomes interesting because the player possesses an interpretation rather than waiting for Gary to give the canonical one.

Gary's thesis is a **character worldview**, not the game's omniscient narrator.

The same applies to Tabitha and Pothole.

---

# 20. Choice quality needs to remain dialogue-first

The current proposal sometimes describes choices abstractly:

> choose Pothole's strategy  
> choose Tabitha's strategy  
> choose Gary's strategy  
> coordinate them  
> sabotage them

Those are useful structural outcomes.

They should almost never be literal option labels.

The earlier playtest found the better interaction balance:

> **dialogue baseline + passive physical staging + map-level agency + sparse decisive intervention.**

And choices are strongest when several options are genuinely defensible:

| Weak | Stronger |
|---|---|
| Support protest / oppose protest | Go publicly / help privately / warn someone first / stay away |
| Agree with Gary / disagree | Give him the document / challenge his claim / ask him not to publish yet |
| Support Tabitha | Stand beside her when the cameras arrive |
| Sabotage Pothole | Give another candidate information he trusted you with |
| Romance response | Keep the invitation / return it / make a joke / avoid answering |

High-semantic choices should continue to do most of the work.

---

# 21. Joined adventures must obey the companionship finding

This is non-negotiable now.

The failed prototype established that:

> NPC moves to next waypoint → player catches up → beat happens

does **not** feel like companionship.

When the player joins the heroes, they need reciprocal causal exchange. Tabitha or another hero acts; the player responds; the hero adapts; the player can initiate or redirect; the group behaves as a social unit.

And the v006b result demonstrated the stronger map function:

> **position yourself in relation to an unfolding human situation.**

Approach, remain, accompany, withdraw, intercept, stay out, cross a threshold.

So the heroic adventure should not become a procession of moving quest-givers.

When they're travelling together, **they travel together** unless separation is itself the decision.

---

# 22. The climax concept is good; the ending currently isn't emotionally finished

"Can they make the victory stick?" is a far more intelligent final problem than defeating a villain.

Keep it.

But implementation and institutional durability can become dramatically dry very quickly.

The public breakthrough should probably function as the **external climax**.

Then the final movement should convert durability into human questions:

Does Gary actually hand authority away?

Does Tabitha accept responsibility for something done under her banner?

Does Lord Pothole take an office whose constraints might destroy the thing people loved?

Does the player remain with one of them?

Does the coalition survive?

What happens to the first place they tried to save?

What ordinary thing do these people do the morning after becoming historically important?

The ending then needs three kinds of closure, echoing an earlier excellent idea:

| Closure | Question |
|---|---|
| **Material** | What actually changed? |
| **Public** | What does Britain think happened? |
| **Personal** | What did it cost these people, and where are they now? |

I would strongly prefer a **final lived scene plus short contextual epilogue** over a wall of policy-ending slides.

---

# 23. Replayability is unusually strong

This part fits the existing design almost perfectly.

The campaign should contain memorable landmark situations whose **identity stays recognisable while their meaning changes**.

The scenario-identity work already established:

> preserve the landmark; vary its route, state, interpretation and consequence.

That is precisely what this storyline needs.

An election night can recur every campaign.

But perhaps:

you're dating Pothole;

you hate Pothole;

you supplied the evidence that made the election possible;

Tabitha isn't speaking to Gary;

Gary has endorsed someone else;

you never joined the movement and are working behind the bar where the results are being watched.

**Same iconic event. Completely different story.**

That's excellent replay architecture.

---

# 24. One major narrative layer is simply missing: sound and music

The visual grammar is already strong.

The trio could benefit enormously from musical narrative identity.

Not necessarily three obvious character jingles. More sophisticatedly:

Pothole's ceremonial motif;

Tabitha's harsher contemporary/post-punk language;

Gary's urgent rhythmic material;

a coalition theme created by combining elements of all three;

fracture versions where they no longer harmonically fit;

private stripped-down versions during romance;

a completely sincere full version at a major victory.

Likewise, ordinary Britain needs its own sonic identity: trains, pubs, rain, gulls, badly insulated flats, community halls, television news, crowds, seaside machines, distant traffic.

This isn't urgent system design yet, but for the desired **epic + intimate + diorama** tone it will eventually be an important narrative channel.

---

# What I would change before designing the first expedition

The current historical research is sufficient. I would stop researching mechanisms of political success for now.

The next narrative pass should make these corrections:

1. **Declare the trio the headline protagonists and the player the experiential protagonist.** The campaign must intersect player-owned desires instead of operating mainly through invitations.
2. **Replace fixed character lessons with open arc questions.**
3. **Design trio chemistry before national plot detail:** each pair's affection, resentment, dependence, private humour and history.
4. **Create a recurring opposing/supporting ensemble** capable of challenging the heroes intelligently.
5. **Define what each hero actually loves about Britain**, then let the player experience those things before threatening them.
6. **Use the historical theory only as a hidden plausibility model.** Never turn campaign capacity into overt mechanics.
7. **Keep the campaign's landmark structure flexible**, and foreshadow every political window.
8. **Restore ordinary life, warmth, beauty, romance and recovery between political set pieces.**
9. **Make dialogue-first, first-pass-comprehensible scene writing a hard quality gate.**
10. **Design the ending around material + public + personal closure, not policy implementation alone.**

After those are solved, I think we can design the first expedition properly.

## Bottom line

The current proposal is **not too ambitious**. The ambition is one of its strongest qualities.

Its problem is almost the opposite: the research has made the **political machine** more developed than the **human story living inside it**.

The strongest version of *Managed Decline* is not merely a historically plausible story about three parody figures building a successful outsider movement.

It is a story where the player eventually cares desperately about **these three ridiculous people**, understands why they care about the country, has lived an ordinary life alongside their extraordinary one, has loved or betrayed some of them, has watched their private selves collide with the public symbols they created—and then discovers that the absurd promise made near the beginning of the game might actually come true.

If we get that balance right, **"rescue Britain" becomes both the epic plot and the mechanism that puts these characters under enough pressure to reveal who they really are.**

# Revised core proposition

> **Three impossible heroes set out to save Britain. The country laughs—until they start winning. You are living your own life while it happens. You can become part of their legend, love them, oppose them, exploit them, or mostly live with what their adventure does to the country around you.**

The critical distinction is now explicit: **Lord Pothole, Tabitha and the Gary parody are the headline/public protagonists; the player is the experiential protagonist.** The trio gives the game its memorable mythic foreground, but the campaign does not own the player's life.

The player should therefore never feel like a fourth member waiting for the next hero quest. Their own work, relationships, money, ambitions, routines, places and private problems remain valid sources of story. The heroic campaign becomes powerful when it collides with those things.

# The story should operate on three coupled planes

| Plane | What it contains | What the player should feel |
|---|---|---|
| **The player's life** | work, friends, romance, housing, money, routines, projects, private ambitions | “This is happening to my life.” |
| **The trio's life together** | friendship, rivalry, attraction, loyalty, dependence, public/private identity, possible fracture | “I know these people.” |
| **The national campaign** | grievances, institutions, evidence, organisation, leverage, elections, courts, policy and structural change | “This ridiculous thing may actually matter.” |

Connective scenes can live mostly on one plane. Major character episodes should usually connect two. The strongest landmarks should make all three collide, because that is where the epic political story becomes personally consequential without turning the player's ordinary life into filler.

# The thematic design should use two intertwined questions

The political question remains:

> **Who owns Britain, who is Britain for, and who gets to decide?**

But the more personal question should become equally important:

> **What happens when a person turns themselves—or is turned by others—into a public symbol powerful enough to change reality? Can the symbol serve the person and the cause without eventually consuming them?**

This fits all three parodies unusually well. Lord Pothole lives through an authored persona. Tabitha was turned into an institutional character and then an internet icon. Gary's authority depends heavily on a repeated autobiographical public story. Their political power and their private vulnerability therefore come from the same source.

Romance, fame, satire, burnout, authenticity and national politics can now explore one underlying dramatic problem instead of feeling like separate systems.

# Replace fixed character arcs with open dramatic questions

The parody invariants remain non-negotiable. What changes is the assumption that each hero has one correct lesson to learn.

| Hero | Open arc question | Possible forms of development without prescribing the answer |
|---|---|---|
| **Lord Pothole** | **What happens when the joke acquires genuine responsibility?** | He may discover formidable civic competence, become trapped as a harmless mascot, reject power to preserve independence, or wield office through the same absurd ceremonial form. |
| **Tabitha** | **What happens when a symbol she never fully controlled becomes powerful enough that other people's lives depend on what it means?** | She may broaden a coalition, clarify harder boundaries, become more responsible without becoming ideologically tame, harden under attack, withdraw from the image, or learn to govern a movement she can no longer personally control. |
| **Gary parody** | **What happens when explaining the system is no longer the same thing as changing it?** | He may learn to distribute authority, become increasingly centralised, work effectively with specialists, burn himself out trying to remain indispensable, or win while still retaining many of the habits that caused conflict. |

The player's relationship may influence those trajectories but should never function as ideological therapy. Dating Tabitha must not secretly be the route that fixes her politics; dating Gary must not teach him to become a polite technocrat; loving Pothole must not reveal that the persona was merely a mask.

# Their political specialties become instincts, not RPG classes

Pothole still has exceptional talent for political theatre and public framing. Tabitha still has unusual power to create identification and mobilisation. Gary still sees structural and strategic relationships others miss.

But none of those capabilities is exclusive. Tabitha can understand a structural connection first. Gary can be unexpectedly good at moving a room. Pothole can quietly perform months of exact procedural work. Their signature abilities should predict what they instinctively try under pressure, not define the only useful action each character can perform.

That keeps the trio recognisable without making every problem solvable by selecting the correct hero.

# Trio chemistry becomes a first-class narrative requirement

Before a major strategic fracture can matter, the player must understand why these people actually keep choosing one another.

| Pair | Affection / admiration to establish | Productive friction |
|---|---|---|
| **Pothole ↔ Tabitha** | Both survive being laughed at, misrepresented or treated as socially unacceptable. Pothole can give her an almost absurd formal dignity when everyone else approaches her as a safeguarding problem; she can enjoy puncturing his ceremony without demanding that he abandon it. | She may regard theatre as evasive when moral lines matter; he may think her willingness to accept an opponent's frame gives those opponents too much power. |
| **Pothole ↔ Gary** | Both are self-authorising outsiders who believe conventional gatekeepers have missed something obvious. Gary can admire Pothole's ability to compress a sprawling issue into something everybody remembers; Pothole can respect Gary's refusal to accept a cosmetic victory. | Gary wants causal precision and may consider Pothole's simplification irresponsible. Pothole may think Gary can explain a problem for forty minutes without giving anybody a sentence they can actually carry into public life. |
| **Tabitha ↔ Gary** | Both are energised by institutional condescension and capable of making people who feel ignored believe that politics might concern them. Each has something the other lacks: she can make people move before they have absorbed a model; he can reveal the machinery underneath an immediate grievance. | Their disagreement over nation, migration, belonging and material distribution can threaten the exact coalition they are trying to build. Neither should be written as the designated adult. |

The trio also needs mundane shared history: recurring rituals, private jokes, bad journeys, things they always order, embarrassing stories, favourite arguments, small acts of care and memories of disasters that nobody outside the group understands. The exact rituals should emerge from authored scenarios rather than a generic friendship system.

A crucial test is whether one hero will sometimes protect or help another when doing so has **no strategic benefit at all**. That is where coalition turns into friendship.

# Add a recurring counterforce ensemble instead of a single villain

The national story needs intelligent people capable of resisting the heroes for understandable reasons. These characters should move between ally and opponent depending on the situation.

| Counterforce role | Narrative function |
|---|---|
| **Competent institutional reformer** | Knows what implementation actually requires, can expose heroic over-simplification, and may genuinely share much of the desired outcome. |
| **Political operator** | Understands elections, parties, bargaining and co-option; can offer a compromise that is materially useful but dangerous to the movement's identity. |
| **Media/framing actor** | Can make the trio vastly more powerful while creating public versions of them that the actual people cannot control. |
| **Rival outsider or movement** | Competes for some of the same grievances and supporters with a different explanation or remedy; may sometimes cooperate. |
| **Local stakeholder who refuses symbolic ownership** | Forces the heroes to confront the fact that helping somebody does not give the movement the right to turn that person's experience into campaign property. |
| **Institutional insider / specialist** | Possesses evidence, technical competence or legal authority the heroes need, but has obligations and risks of their own rather than existing as an unlockable resource. |

No faction gets monopoly on competence, corruption or good faith. A mocked policy can work. A seemingly heroic campaign demand can have an ugly implementation consequence. An official can be braver than an activist. A cynical politician can occasionally make the least harmful decision. That ambiguity protects the satire from becoming propaganda and gives the player real interpretive work.

# Replace the rigid campaign act structure with flexible landmarks

The political-change research remains useful, but it should govern causality behind the scenes rather than dictate a visible sequence of chapters.

| Landmark | What must be dramatically true | What can vary substantially |
|---|---|---|
| **Convergence** | The three discover through a concrete situation that collaborating can accomplish something none of them could have done alone. | Where it happens, why the player is present, what counts as victory, whether the player helps. |
| **First undeniable leverage victory** | They force some authority to do something it had actually refused to do. Attention becomes demonstrated agency. | Election, court, contract, service decision, strike, institutional standard, etc. |
| **Public transformation** | The trio cease being merely odd individuals and become symbols other actors need to interpret, exploit or oppose. | Media route, scale, which hero becomes most famous, whether the player is publicly associated. |
| **Strategic stress test** | Their source-authentic methods become difficult to reconcile under real stakes. | Split, compromise, temporary separation, unexpected alliance, or successful coordination. A breakup is not mandatory. |
| **Foreshadowed national window** | A previously existing situation changes so that something formerly impossible becomes possible. | Court ruling, election, party fracture, inquiry, scandal, budget, industrial deadline, disclosure. |
| **Breakthrough or historic defeat** | The accumulated campaign is finally tested against a decision capable of changing Britain materially. | What decision, which coalition survives, whether the heroes all back the same outcome. |
| **Human aftermath** | The result reaches ordinary life and the heroes have to live as people after becoming historically consequential. | Relationships, offices, careers, public myths, implementation, civilian life and endings. |

The connective material between these landmarks should remain variable and spacious. There can be weeks of ordinary life, romance, work, small local stories and unrelated pleasures. Some campaign threads go dormant. Some heroes work separately. Some episodes never matter nationally.

Political opportunity must always be seeded before it becomes decisive. The election already exists. The court case is already moving. The governing party's fracture has been visible. The document has a history. The player should think **“that earlier thing suddenly matters”**, not **“the finale event just spawned.”**

# The historical theory of change becomes a hidden narrative plausibility system

The frame/organisation/leverage/remedy/opportunity/implementation model should never become a campaign dashboard. It is a **writer and simulation audit** that answers whether a proposed success is causally earned.

The player should encounter concrete things instead: a lawyer who has finally agreed to act, a local organiser who is exhausted, an unexpected invitation from a minister, a strike ballot, a journalist suddenly taking the story seriously, a candidate who can plausibly win, a draft amendment, a piece of evidence, a venue, a friend who refuses to help again.

If the necessary capacity is absent, the story should not silently create it. The opportunity may be missed, produce only a partial result, or become usable through a different route. But the game should never expose `MOVEMENT POWER 73` or tell the player that they need more leverage points.

# Rewrite the local-episode rule around human relevance

The previous six-residue model was too easy to read as a checklist. Material result, evidence, constituency, public meaning, institutional response and relationship consequence are now **possible residues**, not mandatory products of every episode.

A strong episode first asks:

> **Why does this situation matter to this player's life right now, and why does it matter to the people actually living through it?**

The campaign may enter because the player already needs the place, knows the person, is dating one of the heroes, works inside the institution, wants the job, needs the money, has made a promise, or simply happens to be present. If the player has no current connection, the situation can remain background until one develops rather than becoming an unsolicited quest.

Some episodes should yield decisive evidence. Some should only change a friendship. Some should be hilarious and politically useless. Some should make the heroes realise they should not turn a private person's pain into public material. Some should look inconsequential and return much later. This prevents the campaign from converting Britain into a sequence of resource nodes.

# The player remains free without becoming the secret mastermind

Joining the trio should mean **shared life and shared activity**, not automatic strategic command. The player may sometimes be the person with the decisive relationship, fact, money, access or judgment. At other times the heroes know far more than they do.

When the player joins an expedition, companionship remains sticky. They leave together, walk together, travel together, wait for one another and react to the same changing situation unless separation itself is meaningful. Hero initiative comes through proposals, actions, jokes, mistakes, refusals and changes of mind rather than through becoming moving waypoints.

The player can also remain largely outside. That route is valid only if the player's independent life remains rich: jobs, dates, family, money, homes, friends, hobbies, social worlds and personal ambitions continue to develop. The campaign then reaches them through consequences in the shared world. A civilian player can later become unexpectedly important because of the life they built, but the game should not manufacture a destiny that drags them into the movement anyway.

# Protect the player's right to interpret

No hero should own the canonical explanation of Britain.

Gary can see patterns other people miss, but he must not become the game's solution manual. Tabitha can perceive forms of belonging or humiliation that Gary ignores. Pothole can notice an institutional absurdity whose symbolic importance neither understands. The player may possess evidence none of them has, or may draw the wrong conclusion from perfectly reasonable observations.

The hidden situation network remains authoritative about what is true. The heroes supply **worldviews**. The player constructs a provisional understanding from behaviour, places, documents, news, rumours and dialogue.

Whenever possible, major political ideas should first become **experienced facts** before they become speeches. Show the closed building, changed ownership, missing worker, unaffordable home, functioning public service or unexpectedly successful programme. Then let the characters argue about what it means.

# Restore romance as a full human experience

Romance still intersects the campaign, but political utility is no longer its primary content.

| Hero | Central romantic tension | What must exist outside politics |
|---|---|---|
| **Lord Pothole** | Can intimacy deepen without treating the persona as a mask that love removes? Does the player love the person, the performance, or the inseparable combination? | Private rituals, absurd tenderness, ordinary dates, practical care, attraction, embarrassment, periods where nothing historic is happening. |
| **Tabitha** | Can somebody know her without simply becoming another person projecting an idea onto her? Can she know the player without turning them into ally, audience or proof? | Playfulness, desire, friends, music/subculture, private boredom, jealousy and affection unrelated to strategy, disagreement that does not automatically become political debate. |
| **Gary parody** | Can he have a relationship in which the other person is not an audience, recruit, critic to defeat or refuge between campaign sessions? | Humour, domesticity, ambition, exhaustion, sex/affection, hobbies, arguments over time, and moments where the campaign is not allowed to consume the evening. |

Multiple simultaneous relationships remain possible through authored boundaries. Openly negotiated non-monogamy, ambiguity, political convenience and deception are distinct social states. Public scandal is separate from whether the people privately consider an arrangement acceptable.

# Make Britain emotionally worth saving

Every major region or long storyline should establish something genuinely pleasurable before asking the player to care about its loss or transformation.

The game needs working institutions as well as broken ones, beautiful public spaces as well as dereliction, hilarious local customs, landscapes, ordinary kindness, music, food, good pubs, strange clubs, old architecture, wealthy environments that are genuinely lovely, friendships and routines that the player would miss.

Each hero also needs concrete things they personally love about Britain. Those should be shown through behaviour rather than announced in speeches. A hero who wants to save a country becomes far more convincing when the player has actually spent time with them enjoying something inside it.

# Permit sincere heroism and catharsis

Do not automatically puncture every epic moment with an anti-climax. The wet-coat aftermath is useful when it reveals character, but it cannot become a reflexive apology for sincerity.

Sometimes Pothole should look magnificent and the scene should end on that image. Sometimes Tabitha should pull off something genuinely brave. Sometimes Gary should make an argument that changes the room. Sometimes the coalition should win and the music should be allowed to swell.

The satire comes from specificity, contradiction and consequence. It does not require the story to wink immediately after every emotional payoff.

# Scene-writing quality gates become stricter

| Gate | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **First-pass clarity** | The player understands the immediate situation, who wants what and what decision is live without replaying the scene. Subtext may remain difficult; basic comprehension may not. |
| **One dominant live question** | A focused scene can contain complexity, but it should normally have one immediately legible dramatic question rather than six policy topics competing for attention. |
| **High-semantic choices** | Dialogue, silence and decisive acts should express stance, risk or commitment. Do not decompose one emotional intention into five low-value clicks. |
| **No abstract strategy menu** | “Support Gary's plan” is a writer description, not usually a player-facing choice. The player gives him the document, refuses the meeting, stands beside Tabitha, phones the minister, stays home, etc. |
| **Characters act, not only explain** | A political scene should contain behaviour, pressure, interruption, physical context or an immediate decision—not three essays delivered by avatars. |
| **Player inference survives** | Do not use a hero to explain a connection the player could enjoy discovering unless that character's interpretation itself is the dramatic event. |
| **Companionship is reciprocal** | Shared travel and activities contain repeated causal exchange and allow player initiation or redirection. |
| **Aftermath exists** | Major exposure, victory, humiliation, loss or intimacy receives reaction and recovery before unrelated escalation. |
| **Presentation stays dialogue-first** | Focused VN handles semantic density; the diorama handles presence, route, witnessing and aftermath; physical actions are sparse and decisive. |

This keeps the strongest result from the prototype programme: the baseline felt richest when the interaction stayed narratively dense and the player was allowed to infer motives rather than operate an explicit model.

# The ending should close four things

The national breakthrough remains the **external climax**, but it should not be the final emotional beat.

| Closure | Ending question |
|---|---|
| **Material Britain** | What actually changed in services, ownership, institutions, places or people's lives? |
| **Public mythology** | What does the country believe happened, and which version of the heroes survived in collective memory? |
| **The trio** | Are they still friends, allies, rivals, office-holders, burnt-out symbols, lovers, enemies or something stranger? |
| **The player's life** | Who are they with, what life did they build, what did participation or refusal cost them, and what remains personally important after history moves on? |

A short epilogue can show durability over time, but the final substantial scene should preferably be **lived and human**: a familiar place, journey, meal, home, pub, station, coast or other space whose meaning has changed. It should allow the player to inhabit the result rather than read a policy report.

# Revised first-expedition direction

**The Last Bus Home** remains a promising candidate, but it needs to be rebuilt around these narrative rules before prototyping.

| Element | Revised requirement |
|---|---|
| **Entry** | The player already has a personal reason to use or encounter the bus route. They are not summoned by a `SAVE THE BUS` quest. |
| **Hero introduction** | Each hero is encountered doing something recognisable to their parody, not explaining their class role. None has the complete truth. |
| **Shared activity** | If the player joins, they actually make a journey with the trio and the affected people. The route itself gives time for chemistry, observation, ordinary Britain and changing plans. |
| **Live dramatic question** | The immediate problem is simple enough to understand on first encounter even if the underlying institutional cause is complex. |
| **Player ownership** | The player can help, oppose, take a limited role, prioritise the person they were already meeting, or simply try to get home. None is presented as failure to engage with content. |
| **Political consequence** | A small real result can occur, but the episode does not need to manufacture every campaign resource. A later connection to a larger pattern may be only a hint. |
| **Human consequence** | The player should leave knowing something memorable about the trio and at least one ordinary person or place affected by the route. |
| **Heroic beat** | The story is allowed one moment in which the absurd trio genuinely looks like a heroic party without immediately apologising for it. |
| **Aftermath** | Returning to the route later visibly and socially reveals what actually happened. |

The most important prototype question becomes broader than whether the mechanics work:

> **After one complete expedition, do I want to spend more time with these three people—and does their enormous claim that they might save Britain feel slightly less impossible than it did before?**

That is the correct first proof for the new premise.

# Revised design position

The political-process research remains valuable, but it is now explicitly **scaffolding rather than the foreground story**.

The foreground is three unmistakable parody characters becoming unexpectedly formidable and unexpectedly important; the relationships between them; the player's own life repeatedly colliding with theirs; and a Britain that is sufficiently funny, beautiful, comfortable, unfair, frustrating and loved that attempting to save it can be both satirical and sincere.

The campaign mechanics answer **why victory is plausible**.

The narrative design must answer **why the player cares who wins, what it costs, and whether these people still recognise themselves when the country finally starts taking them seriously**.

That is the version I would carry forward.
