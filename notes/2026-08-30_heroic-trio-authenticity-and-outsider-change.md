## Assistant

Yes. This could give **Managed Decline** a much stronger central hook—provided the trio are treated as **self-appointed heroes with incompatible ideas of what “rescuing Britain” means**, rather than a conventional JRPG party that is unquestionably saving the world.

A strong version of the premise is:

> **Three would-be heroes set out to rescue Britain. You can join them, date them, derail them—or just get on with your shift.**

That communicates the characters, the dramatic movement, the satire, the romance and the unusual player freedom in one sentence.

## Why this is stronger

Until now, one of the risks has been that *Managed Decline* can sound like a fascinating simulation without an immediately graspable story: live somewhere, meet people, encounter satire, and gradually become involved. The heroic trio gives the game an unmistakable foreground.

They provide:

- a recognisable cast for the cover, trailers and key art;
- an authored dramatic spine connecting otherwise separate scenarios;
- a reason to travel through different parts of Britain;
- recurring interpersonal conflict rather than disconnected encounters;
- a clear invitation for the player without declaring the player the chosen one;
- a way for the larger simulation to continue around a memorable story.

Most importantly, it could solve the recent problem where the player felt as though they were **chasing Tabitha while she conducted her own life**. When the player accepts an expedition, the characters should genuinely form a temporary travelling group: leaving together, waiting for one another, responding to the same obstacles and sharing an objective. The player should feel they are undertaking something with them.

## The trio should be an unstable heroic coalition

They should not simply be three lovable people who agree about everything. Their incompatible interpretations of Britain are what generate the story.

### Lord Pothole: the myth

He treats neglected public life as something demanding courage, ceremony and heroic intervention. He can turn an abandoned underpass or broken pedestrian bridge into the site of an epic stand.

His strengths are bravery, symbolic clarity and the ability to make people believe an apparently hopeless action matters.

His weaknesses are theatricality, strange priorities and the temptation to substitute a magnificent gesture for a durable result.

### Tabitha: the human situation

She understands people, social openings, appearances and immediate emotional reality. She may recognise that the official “problem” is not what the people affected actually care about.

Her strengths are adaptability, personal connection and the ability to make an adventure feel intimate rather than ideological.

Her weaknesses should include self-presentation, mixed motives and the possibility that she changes position depending on the audience or relationship.

### The Gary parody: the material explanation

He sees the contracts, asset ownership, incentives, rents, budgets and concentrations of power beneath every supposedly isolated failure.

His strengths are diagnosis, strategic focus and an unwillingness to accept official nonsense.

His weaknesses are certainty, escalation and the danger of treating people as examples in an argument he has already completed.

That produces a useful triangle:

- Lord Pothole asks, **“What brave act must be performed?”**
- Tabitha asks, **“What do these people actually need from us?”**
- Gary asks, **“Who owns this, who profits, and why will it happen again?”**

Each is sometimes right. Each can also make a situation significantly worse. The player is not choosing between good, evil and joke options; they are deciding whose account of the situation to strengthen, combine or undermine.

## “Rescuing Britain” should consist of specific rescues

The game should not turn into a generic plot about defeating a prime minister, corporation or supernatural embodiment of decline. Britain is too complicated to be rescued by defeating one villain.

Instead, the trio repeatedly attempts to rescue one painfully specific thing:

- the final night bus serving an estate;
- a library undergoing “temporary service transformation”;
- a seaside arcade being converted into investment accommodation;
- a community centre that has already been sold three times on paper;
- a flood barrier whose maintenance responsibility has disappeared between contractors;
- a public footpath blocked by a development that technically has not begun;
- a group of tenants being moved for a regeneration launch photograph.

Every episode can feel like a miniature heroic adventure while remaining rooted in recognisable material conditions. Occasionally, they should actually achieve something meaningful. If every expedition is merely a joke ending in failure, the characters will become irritating and the player will stop caring.

The larger arc is then not “Can they literally save the whole country?” It is:

> Can these three remain together long enough to discover what saving anything would actually require?

## The player’s relationship to the adventure is the real central mechanic

The player should not choose a permanent mode from a menu. Their position should emerge from what they repeatedly do.

### Join them

The player receives a diegetic invitation at a clear departure moment. Once accepted, the group travels together. The trio should not run ahead while the player follows quest markers.

The player can contribute practical knowledge, social access, local relationships or simply another pair of hands. They need not become party leader immediately.

### Love one or more of them

Romance should change the adventure rather than exist in a separate dating interface.

A relationship can affect:

- who confides their real motive before an expedition;
- who asks the player to support them during an argument;
- whether public decisions become private betrayals;
- who stays after the heroic pose has ended and everyone is cold, wet and exhausted;
- whether the player is regarded as a comrade, an escape route, a source of legitimacy or someone worth changing for.

Multiple simultaneous relationships could work, but they need authored character boundaries. An openly negotiated arrangement, an ambiguous overlap and deliberate deception should not be treated as the same state. Each character should react according to their own values rather than through a universal jealousy meter.

### Derail them

Sabotage should usually appear as a plausible action rather than a red **SABOTAGE** option.

The player might:

- withhold useful information;
- tip off the institution they are confronting;
- encourage the least workable plan;
- turn two party members against one another;
- redirect public attention toward the wrong issue;
- take credit for the result;
- help a supposedly antagonistic local character whose interests conflict with the trio.

The best sabotage may even be defensible. The trio can be wrong, intrusive or dangerously confident.

### Carry on without them

This is one of the most distinctive promises and should be honoured properly.

The player might work, maintain relationships, explore local venues and deal with the consequences of the trio’s adventures without joining them. They hear rumours, encounter stranded participants, see altered locations and meet people affected by the result.

The campaign continues without waiting indefinitely for the player. But declining it must not mean receiving an inferior fragment of the game. It should provide a different vantage point: the people living in the place where someone else is attempting to manufacture history.

The same scenario assets can support both routes. While the trio is trying to “save the last bus,” the civilian player might be working at the pub where passengers arrive after the service collapses.

## A useful rule for every adventure

Each major expedition should produce three separate outcomes:

1. **What materially happened**  
   Did the bus run? Did the building remain open? Who was displaced? What visibly changed on the map?

2. **What people believe happened**  
   Who received credit? What did the news, local gossip, posters and social media turn it into?

3. **What happened between the characters**  
   Who trusted whom? Who felt used? Who became closer? Who privately doubts the entire project?

This creates the right form of satire. The heroes might win the public story but materially fail. They might lose humiliatingly in public while quietly protecting someone. The player may save the practical situation while destroying the party.

It also gives choices visible consequences without needing prominent relationship bars or system messages.

## The dramatic poses are valuable—but as visual punctuation

The supplied image works because the standing and crouching figures immediately promise:

- a relationship between contrasting personalities;
- forward movement;
- danger beneath their feet;
- vulnerability despite the dramatic presentation;
- a tactile miniature world.

For *Managed Decline*, borrow that **visual grammar**, not the precise character poses or composition.

A three-character tableau could use:

- Lord Pothole standing upright and theatrically immovable;
- Tabitha compressed forward, alert and ready to move;
- Gary braced against something, looking as though he is confronting the entire mechanism holding the scene together.

Place them on a recognisably British object that is both heroic and pathetic:

- the roof of the final bus;
- a temporary footbridge above a flooded underpass;
- the remains of a ceremonial regeneration display;
- scaffolding around a condemned community building;
- a traffic island surrounded by newly installed barriers;
- a collapsed public-information pavilion.

The contrast is the joke: **they are posed like mythic heroes, but the epic battlefield is a failed piece of ordinary infrastructure.**

These poses should appear at chapter openings, departures, confrontations and occasional aftermaths. They should not pose constantly. The private scene after the tableau—wet coats, an argument over directions, someone admitting they do not know what to do—is what makes the heroic image emotionally effective.

The character silhouettes also need to remain readable at the small diorama scale. Their stance, coat shape, movement rhythm and idle posture should identify them before facial detail does.

## A suitable first story arc

A season-length campaign could follow this shape:

### The formation

A local failure brings all three characters to the same place for different reasons. Their accidental collaboration produces a real, surprising success. This convinces them that they might actually rescue Britain.

### The tour

They move between several locations, accumulating followers, enemies, attention and increasingly ambitious plans. The player can become a regular member, occasional ally, lover, critic or distant observer.

### The capture

Institutions, media figures, campaign organisations and commercial interests begin trying to adopt, fund, brand, discredit or redirect them. Their public image diverges from what they are doing.

### The fracture

Their different theories of rescue become incompatible. Romance can intensify the division, but the split should not be caused only by romantic jealousy. It should concern credit, compromise, risk and who is being asked to pay for their heroism.

### The final rescue

They face a situation large enough that none of their methods works alone. The player can reunite them, choose one interpretation of rescue, exploit the fracture, help local people bypass the heroes entirely, or leave.

The ending then answers what the player chose to preserve: the heroic legend, a relationship, a particular place, a political possibility, personal stability or nothing at all.

## The best first prototype would be one complete expedition

A strong vertical slice would be **The Last Bus Home**.

A route serving an isolated estate is being withdrawn after its final evening service. The withdrawal is presented publicly as a modern transport transition. The trio decides, for three different reasons, that this is their first great rescue.

Lord Pothole wants to physically lead the bus through a road closure. Tabitha knows the passengers, staff and local political relationships. Gary has traced the contract and believes the withdrawal is the beginning of a larger asset transfer.

The player can:

- board the adventure and travel with them;
- support one plan or create a hybrid plan;
- have a private relationship scene during the journey;
- quietly help the operator or council prevent their intervention;
- encourage a fracture within the group;
- go to work and experience the stranded passengers arriving later;
- see a visibly different transport stop, street and neighbourhood afterward.

The climactic tableau could place the trio on or beside the final bus at a flooded or obstructed crossing. The heroic staging would be sincere, absurd and specific to this game.

That single prototype could test whether the premise genuinely works:

- Did joining feel like travelling together rather than chasing autonomous characters?
- Was declining the invitation a satisfying route rather than missed content?
- Did each member of the trio contribute something indispensable?
- Did romance expose private motives rather than interrupt the story?
- Could the player derail the expedition without selecting an obviously villainous option?
- Was the resulting change visible in the resumed world?
- Did the player understand why the incident mattered without being given a system-forward objective announcement?

## Recommendation

Make Lord Pothole, Tabitha and the Gary parody the **headline heroic trio and the authored spine of the game**. Do not make their adventure the whole game, and do not automatically make the player their leader.

They are the protagonists of a story occurring inside the world. The player chooses how close to that story they stand.

That is considerably stronger than having three important romanceable characters who primarily wait to be encountered. It gives them momentum, makes their relationships with one another as important as their relationships with the player, gives the diorama maps a reason to exist as journeys, and creates a clear dramatic promise without sacrificing the freedom that makes *Managed Decline* unusual.

# First: authenticity has to come before our invented character development

I agree strongly on this.

There should effectively be **two layers to every hero**:

| Layer | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Parody skeleton** | Immediately recognisable source behaviour, appearance, language, preoccupations, contradictions, methods and cultural context |
| **Managed Decline person** | The private life, courage, vulnerability, abilities, relationships, surprising competencies and character development that we invent |

The second layer cannot overwrite the first.

That distinction is particularly important because these three parody targets are radically different phenomena.

### Lord Pothole / Count Binface

Count Binface isn't simply “funny politician”. His real act has a very particular construction: impossible cosmic self-importance combined with hyper-specific British complaints and occasional completely serious propositions.

His 2024 manifesto jumps between water bosses being made to swim in polluted rivers, working train Wi-Fi, building an affordable house, linking ministers' pay to nurses' pay, requiring MPs to have lived in their constituencies, Ceefax and moving a badly positioned pub hand-dryer. ([count-binface-2024.squarespace.com](https://count-binface-2024.squarespace.com/manifesto?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

And there really is sincerity underneath it. Jon Harvey has talked about encouraging people to value voting, while homelessness became a serious part of the Binface campaign. ([bigissue.com](https://www.bigissue.com/news/politics/count-binface-london-mayor-election-manifesto-democracy/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)) In the 2024 London election he received **24,260 actual votes**, finishing above Britain First. ([londonelects.org.uk](https://www.londonelects.org.uk/sites/default/files/2024-06/Results%20Factsheet%202024.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

So Lord Pothole shouldn't become a conventional noble comic-relief knight.

His heroism should remain **Binface-shaped**.

He can stand before a genuinely disastrous national infrastructure problem and announce an absurdly precise decree concerning a pedestrian crossing in Swindon. Everyone assumes he has missed the point. Then, rather unexpectedly, that tiny absurdity exposes the entire institutional failure.

And when things become genuinely frightening, **he does not remove the helmet and become Serious Jon**.

He remains Lord Pothole.

That's important.

His ridiculous persona *is how he confronts power*.

# Gary needs to remain extremely Gary

My previous “structural explanation guy” was far too generic.

Gary Stevenson's actual method is unusually suitable for a game protagonist because he already thinks in terms of a long campaign.

His recurring proposition is intentionally simple: increasing wealth inequality is impoverishing ordinary people; economic debate misunderstands this; the public needs to understand it; and political pressure must eventually make wealth taxation unavoidable. His own description of his project is essentially **explain it simply → ordinary people distribute it → force the issue into mainstream political conversation**. ([garyseconomics.github.io](https://garyseconomics.github.io/transcripts/transcript/im-back-this-is-how-we-fix-the-uk/))

More importantly, he doesn't currently describe electoral office as the obvious route. In 2025 he said he thought the realistic route to changing British economic policy ran through pressuring a major governing party while still talking to politicians across parties. ([garyseconomics.github.io](https://garyseconomics.github.io/transcripts/transcript/im-back-this-is-how-we-fix-the-uk/))

That gives his parody a very specific heroic method.

The Gary parody shouldn't say:

> “Capitalism is bad. Let's help these tenants.”

He should be the maddening bloke saying that the broken laundrette, council sell-off, £2.80 ice cream, your landlord, the Chancellor's fiscal rule and somebody's grandmother losing her house are **the same fucking story**, drawing numbers on whatever surface is nearby while everyone tells him to shut up.

Sometimes he is catastrophically socially inappropriate.

Sometimes everyone thinks he has reduced a deeply personal problem to one of his diagrams.

And sometimes the player gradually realises:

**oh shit, Gary was right about this six hours ago.**

His epic ability isn't punching people.

It's seeing the board.

**Pathways Amelia + the completely unintended internet mythology built around Amelia.**

Officially, *Pathways* is a Prevent-funded educational package about extremism and radicalisation. ([shoutoutuk.org](https://www.shoutoutuk.org/pathways/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)) Amelia is the anti-immigration activist character who attempts to draw Charlie into political activism and protest. ([knowyourmeme.com](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/amelia-pathways?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

Then something extraordinary happened. The intended cautionary character was appropriated online as an attractive anti-establishment nationalist heroine, resulting in extensive memes and AI-generated heroic depictions. ([theguardian.com](https://www.theguardian.com/politics/2026/jan/25/ai-generated-british-schoolgirl-becomes-far-right-social-media-meme?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

That accidental transformation should be **central to Tabitha's parody**.

She should absolutely not become merely “cute goth girl who cares about people.”

She is someone the authorities have already decided is dangerous, whose notoriety paradoxically makes her more appealing, who has an instinct for protest and political confrontation, and whose nationalism and immigration views create genuine ideological conflict.

But *Managed Decline* can invent the human being that the original educational game never bothered to construct.

Perhaps she is extraordinarily loyal.

Perhaps she physically cannot tolerate watching someone being bullied by an institution.

Perhaps she is much funnier in person than her online persona suggests.

Perhaps she discovers that people who enthusiastically repost her image frequently don't understand her at all.

Perhaps she hates becoming a symbol.

Or secretly adores it.

Probably both.

Her heroic strength is then **mobilisation**. Tabitha can make people who normally feel politically inert decide that they are going somewhere tonight and actually doing something.

That is a fundamentally different power from Gary's.

# And this is where the historical research gets very interesting

There isn't one mechanism by which outsiders “change the country.”

There are several.

And remarkably, our three characters naturally possess different pieces of the machinery.

Political-movement research usually treats **framing, mobilising resources/organisation and political opportunity** as separate but interacting determinants of whether movements go anywhere. ([doi.org](https://doi.org/doi%3A10.1146/annurev.soc.26.1.611?utm_source=chatgpt.com)) Research also distinguishes winning an immediate demand from becoming accepted inside the policy process or changing underlying social values. ([journals.sagepub.com](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0002716293528001006?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

So imagine the trio not as a JRPG tank/DPS/healer composition but as:

| Hero | What they can do that the others can't |
|---|---|
| **Lord Pothole** | Capture attention, puncture authority, create irresistible political theatre, turn institutional absurdity into a story everybody understands |
| **Tabitha** | Generate identification, followers, participation and direct action; transform resentment into people actually showing up |
| **Gary** | Diagnose structural causes, formulate an intellectually defensible programme, identify leverage and stop the movement winning meaningless concessions |
| **Player** | Connect, moderate, radicalise, betray, organise, finance, humanise or destabilise this combination |

That suddenly gives them a credible pathway to becoming dangerous to the establishment.

# There are historical precedents for almost every stage of their journey

And some are remarkably close to *Managed Decline*.

### Jón Gnarr is probably essential research for Lord Pothole

After Iceland's financial collapse destroyed trust in conventional politics, comedian Jón Gnarr's deliberately absurd **Best Party** won **35% of Reykjavík's vote**, became its largest party, entered coalition and made Gnarr mayor. ([grapevine.is](https://grapevine.is/society/article/2010/06/10/joke-party-wins-elections-in-reykjavik/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

Academic work on the phenomenon is particularly interesting because it argues that Gnarr's political performance refused the distinction between **parody and sincerity**. ([anthrosource.onlinelibrary.wiley.com](https://anthrosource.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/amet.12020?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

That is almost exactly what Lord Pothole needs.

The lesson isn't:

> joke candidate eventually becomes serious.

It is:

> **the joke becomes a legitimate mode of seriousness.**

And it only became electorally explosive because a crisis had created a political opportunity in which normal politicians had forfeited enormous credibility. ([iris.hi.is](https://iris.hi.is/en/publications/a-comedian-who-changed-the-leadership-script-in-reykjavik-the-cas/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

So Lord Pothole could plausibly go from 0.7%-style lovable candidate to actually holding office if the game's evolving Britain creates the right moment.

# Martin Bell demonstrates another route

In 1997, former BBC journalist Martin Bell challenged Neil Hamilton as an independent anti-sleaze candidate in what had been a very safe Conservative constituency.

Labour and the Liberal Democrats **stood aside**. ([independent.co.uk](https://www.independent.co.uk/news/election-97-bell-in-clear-as-tatton-s-lib-dem-pulls-out-of-fray-1267245.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

That's a useful game mechanism:

**the heroes don't necessarily need to build a national party.**

A particular scandal can become so symbolically potent that other actors coordinate around an outsider.

Imagine Lord Pothole unexpectedly becoming the consensus candidate in a ridiculous by-election that was initially intended merely as another stunt.

Suddenly the joke has acquired parliamentary privileges.

# Rashford demonstrates rapid pressure from outside politics

Marcus Rashford's 2020 campaign created enough public and political pressure that the government reversed its position and funded free school meals through the summer, covering about 1.3 million children. ([itv.com](https://www.itv.com/news/2020-06-16/government-u-turns-on-children-s-free-summer-meals-after-rejecting-plea-from-marcus-rashford?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

But research on celebrity campaigning makes an important distinction: celebrity visibility often works because it amplifies campaigns, organisations and demands that already have institutional substance beneath them. ([onlinelibrary.wiley.com](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1467-923X.13446?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

That's highly usable.

Tabitha might create the attention.

Lord Pothole might create the unforgettable image.

Gary might make sure that behind the image sits an actual implementable demand.

Then another organisation, union, barrister, councillor, whistleblower or civil servant gives them the institutional route to make it happen.

They don't magically save something by getting enough retweets.

# Alan Bates gives us the opposite kind of heroic story

The Horizon campaign shows how apparently powerless individuals can eventually defeat an institution that possesses vastly more money, authority and legal resources.

The broad progression was astonishingly long:

people realise their supposedly individual experiences are shared → victims organise → evidence accumulates → 555 claimants pursue group litigation → landmark High Court findings in 2019 → criminal convictions begin falling → television dramatisation massively expands public salience → political pressure accelerates → Parliament legislates to quash hundreds of qualifying convictions in 2024. ([itv.com](https://www.itv.com/news/2024-01-09/who-has-questions-to-answer-over-the-post-office-horizon-scandal?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

That's practically a narrative-design document.

And it shows why our “mini adventures” can matter enormously.

The characters might encounter what appears to be **one person's stupid bureaucratic problem** in chapter two.

Gary notices a pattern.

Tabitha finds six more people.

Lord Pothole turns up outside headquarters carrying an eight-foot ceremonial fax machine.

Nothing seems to happen.

Twenty hours later, evidence discovered in another town connects back to it.

Eventually it becomes one of the cases that can actually bring down an institution.

Now the player's memory matters.

# Even enormous national change doesn't require winning government

This is probably the most important finding.

A challenger can change what the major parties do simply by becoming **electorally dangerous enough**.

The rise of UKIP was one factor putting pressure on David Cameron over an EU referendum; research describes the growing threat to Conservative votes as part of the political context in which the referendum pledge became attractive. ([link.springer.com](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1057/s41269-018-0111-3?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

Whatever one thinks about Brexit, structurally this is enormously important for *Managed Decline*:

> **A movement can succeed without ever becoming the government.**

Gary could therefore be completely right when he argues:

“We don't need 326 MPs. We need enough voters, enough organisation and the right issue so that the people with 326 MPs become frightened of ignoring us.”

Lord Pothole thinks they should stand everywhere.

Tabitha thinks parliamentary politics is compromised anyway.

And suddenly we have a serious argument between three characters whose disagreement follows naturally from their parody sources.

That's far better than giving them arbitrary JRPG personality conflicts.

# So I think the real campaign structure should be much larger

The heroes begin the game already making the outrageous claim:

## **WE ARE GOING TO SAVE BRITAIN.**

Nobody, including perhaps the player, knows exactly what that means.

Their early adventures establish **evidence of agency**. They successfully stop something, expose something, rescue someone, humiliate somebody powerful, win a council vote, lose another fight horribly, attract followers.

Then an important transition occurs.

The individual disasters start connecting.

Housing.

Assets.

Local services.

Migration and social cohesion.

Institutional legitimacy.

Generational inequality.

Political representation.

Infrastructure.

The heroes gradually formulate competing explanations of **what has actually gone wrong with Britain**.

And *that* is where the national story comes from.

Their adventure can then organically pass through something like the following escalation:

**incident → local victory → notoriety → followers → repeated campaigns → recognisable movement → establishment response → national argument → internal fracture over strategy → political opportunity → breakthrough → struggle to make the breakthrough durable.**

That's the one list I would actually put on the wall of the project.

# The “political opportunity” is what we were missing

History doesn't usually work like:

> Heroes try very hard → support number reaches 100 → Britain fixed.

Outsiders frequently spend years accomplishing very little.

Then something changes.

A financial crisis.

A grotesque scandal.

An election nobody expected.

A major institutional failure.

A governing party splits.

A leaked document.

A court decision.

A policy failure becomes impossible to deny.

The established parties suddenly need someone else's voters.

Something that was politically impossible six months earlier becomes extremely possible.

This is what the social-movement literature calls, broadly, **political opportunity**: changes in the environment can radically alter what challengers are capable of achieving. ([annualreviews.org](https://www.annualreviews.org/doi/pdf/10.1146/annurev.soc.30.012703.110545?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

That should become part of the narrative simulation.

The trio spend much of the game unknowingly accumulating:

**credibility + constituency + organisation + evidence + relationships + a coherent story.**

Those assets don't automatically produce victory.

They mean that **when history opens a door, they are capable of walking through it.**

That's how we give them a genuine fighting chance without making the world ridiculously easy to change.

# And it produces much better gameplay

Suppose a spectacular national scandal erupts.

There isn't one “Main Quest: Respond to scandal.”

Lord Pothole says this is their moment to fight a previously unwinnable parliamentary seat.

Tabitha wants 30,000 people in the streets on Saturday.

Gary thinks both are distractions because two Treasury officials are suddenly prepared to discuss legislation that was politically impossible last month.

All three may be right.

The player may:

support one;

somehow make all three work together;

date two of them while making this impossibly complicated;

leak Gary's strategy;

persuade Tabitha not to do something disastrous;

encourage her to do it;

stand as Lord Pothole's campaign manager;

give information to the government;

or go to work and watch the whole extraordinary episode unfold on televisions in the pub.

Now **player agency and national narrative are no longer in conflict**.

# Success shouldn't mean “Britain is perfect”

But they absolutely can **win**.

Research on movements warns that immediate policy victories can later be reversed if the movement hasn't changed the wider coalition and political environment around the issue. ([link.springer.com](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11186-021-09460-2?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

That gives us a much better final-act problem than “defeat final boss.”

They might achieve something genuinely historic:

a new tax settlement;

a housing settlement;

a constitutional reform;

a major transfer of power;

# 1. The authenticity standard

Use this formula for all three:

> **Recognisable public phenomenon + invented private human being + wholly original expression.**

The first part keeps the parody unmistakable. The second gives the character enough depth to carry a long game. The third prevents the parody from becoming a renamed copy.

Each hero should therefore have four authored layers:

| Layer | Function |
|---|---|
| **Parody invariants** | Features that must remain recognisable throughout the game |
| **Generative contradiction** | The tension that produces both admirable behaviour and characteristic failure |
| **Invention zone** | Original biography, skills, vulnerabilities, relationships and growth |
| **False-friend guardrails** | Apparently natural developments that would actually destroy the parody |

A useful test is to remove the character's name from a scene. A player familiar with the source should still recognise the target from the character's method, rhythm, priorities and mistakes—not merely from hair colour or a catchphrase.

A second test is more important: after an intimate ten-hour romance arc, the character should still make decisions that only this parody could make. Emotional depth cannot be represented by temporarily turning them into a sensible generic person.

# 2. Lord Pothole authenticity dossier

## The source phenomenon

Count Binface combines a cosmic fictional identity, absolute deadpan, British electoral ritual, hyper-specific civic irritations and occasional serious policy without clearly marking where the joke ends. His manifestos place plausible reforms beside deliberately absurd decrees. His campaigning also contains a sincere democratic purpose and support for homelessness work.

This is no longer merely theoretical evidence that satirical politics can become consequential. In the Clacton by-election on 13 August 2026, Count Binface received **9,455 votes and 26.93%**, finishing second after the principal national parties declined to field candidates. The unusual field created a political opening in which a candidate normally treated as ceremonial protest became the most substantial alternative on the ballot. That does not mean he had suddenly become a conventional politician. It means the surrounding structure changed the political meaning of the same performance.

That is almost a perfect real-world demonstration of the mechanism Lord Pothole needs.

## Parody invariants

Lord Pothole must preserve all of the following:

- an impossible title, ceremonial identity and self-authored mythology;
- total refusal to apologise for or explain the premise;
- deadpan treatment of tiny British defects as matters of constitutional or interplanetary importance;
- manifestos and speeches that place an excellent policy next to an absurdly precise civic decree;
- participation in elections as public theatre and democratic ritual, including dignified defeat;
- an underlying concern with civic fairness, representation and overlooked people;
- a capacity to make authority look more ridiculous than he does;
- persistence in character when events become emotionally or politically serious.

His voice should use declarative proclamations, exact nouns, procedural language and specific places. He should sound less like a whimsical fantasy knight and more like an extraterrestrial head of state issuing a binding order about a defective pedestrian crossing.

## Generative contradiction

> **Ridicule makes him unusually free, but public demand for ridicule can imprison him.**

Because nobody expects normal behaviour, Lord Pothole can say things conventional politicians cannot say, enter rooms that would reject an ordinary activist, and survive humiliation that would destroy a status-dependent figure. But the moment he develops real power, supporters, journalists and opponents all want him to remain harmlessly entertaining.

His central struggle is not learning to become serious. It is making seriousness happen **through the form everyone has mistaken for a joke**.

## Invention zone

The game can invent an unexpectedly formidable private and heroic side without taking off the helmet metaphorically or literally:

- He remembers obscure places, local grievances and forgotten petitioners with almost frightening precision.
- He is fearless in formal rooms because conventional status cannot be withdrawn from someone who never asked for it.
- Ceremony is how he survives grief, loneliness or humiliation; Jon Harvey has described the real character as connected to processing the sudden death of his brother, which shows how surreal performance can carry sincere emotional weight.
- He can become procedurally competent: nominations, standing orders, coalition negotiation, constituency casework, public records and the practical mechanics of office.
- He may genuinely love Britain as an accumulation of ordinary places rather than as an abstract patriotic slogan.
- His private kindness can still be formal and peculiar. He does not suddenly speak like a therapist.

## Characteristic failures

- He substitutes an unforgettable gesture for an implementable next step.
- He refuses useful compromise because it would damage the ceremonial integrity of the act.
- He attracts people who want a harmless mascot rather than change.
- His policy mixture lets hostile actors dismiss sound proposals by associating them with the absurd ones.
- He becomes so good at surviving defeat that he is slow to recognise a moment when victory is actually possible.

## Arc

Lord Pothole's arc should be:

> **attention → legitimacy → electoral opportunity → responsibility → durable civic competence**

He does not become normal. The surrounding country becomes unable to treat him as politically irrelevant.

A plausible major turning point is a by-election or mayoral contest in which a scandal, party boycott, opposition stand-down or collapse of institutional trust turns him from ceremonial candidate into the only credible vessel for a protest coalition. Jón Gnarr and Martin Bell show two different ways this can happen.

## Romance rule

Intimacy must not be represented as discovering the ordinary man beneath the helmet. The persona is not a false exterior that love cures.

Romance can instead reveal:

- the preparation behind apparent spontaneity;
- the cost of permanent public performance;
- rituals of trust and private ceremonial language;
- practical acts of care;
- grief or fear expressed through the same strange vocabulary;
- whether the player loves him or merely loves access to the legend.

## False-friend guardrails

Do not turn him into:

- a generic comic-relief knight;
- an eccentric aristocrat with a normal personality underneath;
- a nihilist who thinks all politics is fake;
- an incompetent mascot whose only function is to generate publicity;
- a conventional statesman who occasionally remembers to make a joke.

# 3. Tabitha authenticity dossier

## The source phenomenon

Tabitha's target is not merely Amelia as drawn in *Pathways*. It is a three-layer cultural event:

1. **The original instructional character** — a relatively thin anti-immigration activist used inside a Prevent-oriented interactive learning package, inviting Charlie toward activism and protest while the player is asked to evaluate choices and wellbeing.
2. **The institution that framed her** — public-sector safeguarding language, radicalisation pedagogy, branching choices and the attempt to make a politically troublesome young woman legible as a risk pathway.
3. **The unintended mythology** — the purple-haired figure escaped the educational frame and was appropriated online as an attractive nationalist and anti-establishment icon, including AI-generated variants circulated in Britain and elsewhere in Europe.

Tabitha is unmistakable only if the game retains all three layers.

## Parody invariants

Tabitha should remain:

- an adult original character who became notorious through a failed government, council or NGO safeguarding game;
- visually signalled by purple hair as one deliberate wink, while her haircut, face, silhouette, clothing, accessories and animation remain original;
- politically nationalist and sceptical or hostile toward immigration in ways that create real disagreement, rather than merely using vague anti-establishment language;
- instinctively action-oriented, confrontational and capable of drawing somebody into a protest or campaign;
- ambiguous about the boundary between conviction, provocation and trolling;
- attractive to supporters partly because institutional condemnation increases her charisma;
- caught between official caricature, hostile caricature and idealised fan mythology;
- capable of using her symbolic status while resenting other people for reducing her to it.

Her source character has much less developed voice material than Count Binface or Gary Stevenson, so recognisability must come primarily from situation, politics, social effect and cultural afterlife rather than imitation of exact dialogue.

Her voice should be quick, direct, contemporary and socially observant. She can move from flippant banter to a serious accusation without announcing a tonal change. Avoid excessive accent spelling, generic internet slang and long ideological lectures.

## Generative contradiction

> **She wants to defend a real community against people who treat it as disposable, but her definition of the community can exclude people who actually live inside it.**

A second contradiction sits beside it:

> **She hates being turned into a symbol and is very good at exploiting the symbol.**

Those tensions are more authentic and dramatically useful than either sanitising her or writing her as a one-note extremist mascot.

## Invention zone

The game can give Tabitha a genuinely epic human side:

- She is exceptionally brave in crowds and confrontations where more polished people freeze.
- She notices humiliation quickly and cannot tolerate watching an institution bully somebody who cannot speak its language.
- She is fiercely loyal and practically helpful after the cameras leave.
- She knows the physical and social history of her town: demolished estates, renamed buildings, old routes, clubs, industries and family networks.
- She can make politically inert people feel that showing up together tonight might matter.
- She is funny in person and far less solemn than either supporters or opponents expect.
- She is capable of learning logistics, stewarding, volunteer discipline, welfare, security and the unglamorous work that converts a following into an organisation.
- She may privately fear that the people sharing heroic edits of her would dislike the actual woman.

Her heroic power is not generic empathy. It is **identification and mobilisation**: she creates a felt “we,” makes an issue personal and gets bodies into a place.

## Characteristic failures

- She escalates rhetoric or tactics because attention is the only proof of power she trusts.
- She mistakes a very loud online audience for a reliable organisation.
- The movement's projections outrun her actual intentions.
- She humiliates an ally for insufficient loyalty and destroys a wider coalition.
- She can identify institutional contempt accurately while misidentifying its cause.
- She enjoys the force of being feared more than she admits.
- She cannot easily distinguish principled compromise from surrender or betrayal.

## Arc

Tabitha's arc should be:

> **object in somebody else's instructional script → icon owned by strangers → real organiser → author of a movement → person responsible for deciding who belongs inside it**

Growth must not mean quietly deleting the views that make the parody recognisable. It means forcing those views into contact with real people, real consequences, coalition requirements and responsibility for those who act in her name.

## Romance rule

The central romantic question is:

> **Can the player know and love a person after everybody—including the player—has learned to encounter her as a political image?**

A relationship may become a source of legitimacy, scandal, escape, control or genuine refuge. The player can support her, challenge her, use her fame, manage her image or become part of it.

Multiple relationships require character-specific boundaries. Tabitha may respond very differently to an openly negotiated arrangement, public ambiguity and deception. A universal jealousy meter would flatten her.

## False-friend guardrails

Do not turn her into:

- a generic cute goth with vaguely rebellious politics;
- a secretly conventional progressive whose recognisable beliefs disappear when the player knows her better;
- an uncritical nationalist fantasy rewarded by the game;
- a copied Amelia design with a new name;
- a permanent culture-war lecture machine;
- the trio's uncomplicated “human heart,” as though other people and material systems are beneath her notice.

# 4. Gary authenticity dossier

## The source phenomenon

Gary Stevenson's public identity combines a working-class East London origin story, a self-mythologised career as a highly successful Citibank trader, a deliberately simple theory of wealth inequality, an adversarial explanatory style and a campaign strategy built around public economic education and external pressure rather than immediate personal office-holding.

In his own 2025 account, the realistic route was not to stand for Parliament himself but to build enough public force that the governing party would need the movement, while remaining willing to speak to Labour, Greens, Reform, Liberal Democrats and Conservatives. He repeatedly presents inequality as the causal structure connecting declining living standards, asset ownership, housing and political weakness.

His public story also contains disputed territory. Former colleagues have challenged the strongest version of the “best trader in the world” claim, while Stevenson has defended it. The parody should therefore treat the boast as part of the character's self-authorising mythology rather than assert a disputed real-world accolade as neutral fact.

In July 2026 Stevenson stopped producing weekly videos, citing damage to his health after building an audience of roughly 1.6 million. That gives the campaign's cost and leader-dependency unusual contemporary relevance.

## Parody invariants

The Gary parody must preserve:

- a working-class-to-City trader origin used as proof that he has seen the machinery from inside;
- one intentionally simple inequality thesis repeatedly applied to apparently unrelated events;
- numbers, diagrams, household examples, urgency and profanity;
- impatience with expert language that obscures ownership and distribution;
- concentration on taxing wealth and ownership rather than merely raising taxes on work;
- a desire to form a broad working- and middle-class coalition rather than remain inside a comfortable left subculture;
- preference for movement pressure over becoming an ordinary party politician;
- a self-mythologising confidence that is both persuasive and contestable;
- the habit of returning a personal anecdote or isolated service failure to the same structural story.

His voice should accumulate. He makes the same causal point from several directions, interrupts objections before they are complete, uses concrete numbers and occasionally becomes funnier because everybody else already knows which explanation is coming.

Do not reduce this to a catchphrase. The recognisable effect is that the player can predict the thesis but cannot always predict the connection he will draw.

## Generative contradiction

> **He believes radical simplification is necessary to make hidden power visible, but simplification can flatten both technical difficulty and human particularity.**

A second contradiction is equally important:

> **He wants a mass movement rather than a personality cult, but the movement's credibility and reach depend heavily on his own story, confidence and face.**

## Invention zone

The game can invent an epic side grounded in those tensions:

- He can see strategic relationships between events long before the rest of the cast.
- He is unusually good at teaching people who have been made to believe economics is beyond them.
- He mentors younger organisers and gives away useful knowledge without pretending neutrality.
- He will tell allies an unwelcome truth when victory theatre is replacing material change.
- He fears not merely being wrong, but being right early enough to watch the preventable outcome arrive anyway.
- Campaigning can become an addiction through which he avoids ordinary vulnerability.
- He can experience burnout, health damage and terror that stepping back means abandoning everybody who trusted him.
- He may secretly envy Lord Pothole's freedom from factual scrutiny and Tabitha's ability to make people move before they understand every mechanism.

His heroic power is **board-reading and strategic diagnosis**.

## Characteristic failures

- He treats a person in pain as the latest example in an argument he has already won.
- He underestimates implementation specialists, lawyers, administrators or organisers because they do not share his explanatory clarity.
- He assumes correct diagnosis will naturally produce a workable programme.
- He centralises the movement around his own authority while condemning elite centralisation.
- He alienates possible allies by treating uncertainty as cowardice or corruption.
- He lets the campaign consume relationships and health.
- He can be captured by the need to vindicate his biography rather than secure the policy.

## Arc

Gary's arc should be:

> **analyst → broadcaster → movement-builder → coalition strategist → policy negotiator → implementer who accepts distributed expertise and succession**

He does not learn that his central analysis was foolish. He learns that being correct is only one input into political change, and that specialists and shared leadership need not be betrayals of clarity.

## Romance rule

Romance should expose the competition between mission and intimacy. The player can be an intellectual equal, organiser, critic, refuge, source of money or person he keeps turning into an audience.

The relationship should not reduce to calming down the angry economic man. A lover may instead intensify him, challenge his monopoly on strategy, force a succession decision or become entangled in the movement's public credibility.

## False-friend guardrails

Do not turn him into:

- a generic socialist lecturer;
- an omniscient economist who is correct on every specialist question;
- an uncritical dramatisation of his memoir;
- a clown who only rants about billionaires;
- a conventional parliamentary careerist;
- the trio's sober adult whose job is to explain why the other two are silly.

# 5. The authentic heroic coalition

The research supports a very specific division of political capability:

| Hero | Irreplaceable capability | Characteristic blind spot |
|---|---|---|
| **Lord Pothole** | Memorable framing, public theatre, ridicule-proof legitimacy, electoral ritual | A symbol or gesture can displace delivery |
| **Tabitha** | Identification, recruitment, turnout, direct action and movement energy | Attention and in-group loyalty can destroy coalition breadth |
| **Gary** | Structural diagnosis, programme, leverage analysis and resistance to cosmetic concessions | Correctness can substitute for implementation and relationship work |
| **Player** | Connection, translation, logistics, money, insider access, emotional influence, betrayal or refusal | The player may make the coalition possible—or turn every public decision into private capture |

Their recurring questions should be equally specific:

- **Lord Pothole:** What public act makes this failure impossible to ignore?
- **Tabitha:** Who will recognise themselves in this and actually show up?
- **Gary:** What structure caused this, who has the power to change it, and what concession would alter the structure?

Their disagreements should arise from those methods rather than from arbitrary party banter.

For example, Gary may argue that Tabitha's immigration frame divides the exact working- and middle-class coalition required to challenge wealth ownership. Tabitha may argue that Gary asks people to join an economic abstraction while dismissing belonging, borders and cultural loss as distractions. Lord Pothole may reject both versions because neither can be reduced to a civic demand that ordinary people can repeat without a two-hour podcast or a culture-war identity test.

Those are authentic, consequential conflicts. The player cannot resolve them merely by selecting a pleasant dialogue option.

# 6. What outsider success actually looks like

Historical research does not reveal one heroic recipe. It reveals several routes, each requiring a different combination of framing, organisation, leverage, opportunity and implementation.

## Fifteen useful cases

| Case | What happened | Design lesson |
|---|---|---|
| **Count Binface, Clacton 2026** | In an exceptional by-election field without the principal national parties, he received 9,455 votes and finished second. | The same satirical performer can become consequential when the opportunity structure changes. Build capacity before the opening appears. |
| **Jón Gnarr and the Best Party, Reykjavík 2010** | After Iceland's financial crisis discredited conventional politics, the satirical party won about 34.7%, took six of fifteen council seats, formed a coalition and made Gnarr mayor. | Parody and sincerity need not be sequential. Crisis, coalition and governing competence can turn political theatre into office. |
| **Martin Bell, Tatton 1997** | A trusted outsider challenged a scandal-damaged incumbent; Labour and the Liberal Democrats stood aside, allowing Bell to win roughly 60%. | A symbolic scandal plus opposition coordination can create a one-seat insurgent breakthrough without a national party. |
| **Richard Taylor and Kidderminster Hospital, 2001–05** | A retired doctor won Wyre Forest with 28,487 votes on the hospital issue and was re-elected. The original service loss was not simply reversed. | A concentrated grievance and credible messenger can win representation, but winning a seat is not identical to winning the policy. |
| **Marcus Rashford and free school meals, 2020** | Personal testimony, celebrity reach, a precise time-limited demand and parliamentary pressure contributed to a government reversal and a £120 million summer food fund for about 1.3 million children. | Visibility works best when attached to a clear, administratively ready decision that can be made now. |
| **Alan Bates and the Horizon campaign** | Isolated victims found one another, organised, accumulated evidence, brought 555-person group litigation, secured landmark 2019 findings, gained wider media salience and contributed to 2024 legislation quashing eligible convictions. | Pattern recognition, victim organisation, evidence, litigation, storytelling and legislation can form one long causal chain. |
| **Hillsborough families** | Decades of campaigning helped produce the 2012 independent panel, fresh inquests and 2016 unlawful-killing verdicts; further public-accountability reform remained in the legislative process in 2026. | Moral endurance can reopen official truth, but justice, accountability and durable institutional reform are separate stages. |
| **Anti-poll-tax movement, 1989–91** | Mass non-payment, administrative overload, local organisation, protest and elite political conflict made the Community Charge increasingly unsustainable before its replacement. | Distributed non-compliance can be leverage. Do not reduce a complex victory to one dramatic riot. |
| **Ford Dagenham sewing machinists, 1968** | A relatively small group of women in a production choke point stopped work over grading and pay; the dispute became a catalyst for wider equal-pay reform. | A small constituency can matter nationally when it controls a strategic bottleneck and expresses a legible injustice. |
| **Bristol Bus Boycott, 1963** | Campaigners exposed a racial colour bar, built a local coalition and targeted a specific employer through boycott and public pressure; the ban ended and the campaign fed the wider case for race-relations law. | Prove the practice, identify the decision-maker, concentrate pressure and connect a local test case to national legislation. |
| **Save Lewisham Hospital, 2013** | Community mobilisation and the council's legal challenge defeated proposed service changes when courts held that the Secretary of State had exceeded statutory powers. | A crowd creates legitimacy; legal standing and a precise argument can actually transfer the decision. |
| **Living Wage campaign** | Community organising, a credible wage standard, accreditation and anchor institutions diffused the practice across more than 16,000 employers, with the Foundation reporting gains for around half a million workers. | Some change spreads through standards, status, procurement and voluntary institutional adoption rather than one national legislative showdown. |
| **UKIP and the EU referendum pledge** | Electoral threat and Conservative division were among the pressures that made a referendum pledge strategically attractive to the governing party. | A pressure party can win issue adoption without governing—and can lose control of the consequences after a major party takes over the demand. |
| **Britain's 2003 anti-Iraq-war protest** | An estimated one to two million people marched in London, but the invasion proceeded. | Enormous visibility and turnout do not automatically create decision leverage, institutional vetoes, elite defection or control over implementation. |
| **Kinder Scout mass trespass, 1932** | The action became an enduring symbol of access campaigning; major access legislation arrived seventeen years later through a much longer political and organisational chain. | An iconic event can recruit, frame and legitimise a movement without being the direct or immediate cause of legislation. |

# 7. The derived theory of change for Managed Decline

The historical cases and movement literature support a weakest-link model:

> **change capacity = frame × organisation × leverage × executable remedy × political opportunity × implementation durability**

This is not intended as a literal player-facing equation. Its point is that a campaign with a zero in any category can fail despite brilliance elsewhere.

- A million people may agree but possess no leverage.
- A devastating court judgment may arrive without an organisation capable of sustaining public pressure.
- A charismatic movement may enter a policy window with no implementable proposal.
- A government may announce a victory that is never implemented.
- A successful leader may leave behind no succession or durable coalition.

## The twelve-stage conversion chain

A realistic national campaign can move through:

1. **Lived grievance** — somebody encounters a concrete failure.
2. **Pattern recognition** — apparently isolated cases connect.
3. **Evidence and testimony** — the claim becomes defensible.
4. **Diagnostic frame** — the movement explains what caused it.
5. **Prognostic frame** — it identifies a remedy.
6. **Motivational frame** — people acquire a reason to act now.
7. **Constituency and organisation** — supporters become reliable participants.
8. **Leverage** — the movement can impose a cost, move authority or create a veto.
9. **Decision vehicle** — a court application, amendment, executive decision, election, contract, strike demand or institutional standard exists.
10. **Political opportunity** — crisis, scandal, split, election, disclosure or deadline makes the decision newly possible.
11. **Breakthrough and implementation** — somebody with authority acts and the decision reaches material reality.
12. **Durability and succession** — the coalition, law, institution or norm survives backlash and leader exhaustion.

This is the missing process behind the heroic adventure.

# 8. What mini-stories now do

A local episode is not filler and not merely a moral vignette. Every substantial scenario should be capable of producing six kinds of residue:

1. **Local material result** — what happened to the bus, building, tenant, worker or service.
2. **Evidence** — a document, witness, recurring pattern or contradiction that may matter later.
3. **Constituency** — people recruited, alienated, radicalised, exhausted or organised.
4. **Public meaning** — the version that enters news, gossip, memes and political memory.
5. **Institutional response** — concession, surveillance, co-option, legal threat, procedural defence or counter-campaign.
6. **Relationship consequence** — trust, debt, attraction, betrayal, rivalry or a changed interpretation of the player.

An incident encountered in hour two can therefore become evidence in hour twenty. The player who ignored the original victim may later recognise the name in a court bundle. The person whom Tabitha recruited may become a chapter organiser. The councillor Lord Pothole treated graciously after defeat may later be the only insider willing to supply a document. A technical specialist whom Gary insulted may possess the implementation route the movement eventually needs.

That creates continuity without turning every episode into an announced contribution to a progress bar.

# 9. Campaign state should be concrete, not a dashboard

The research does support hidden campaign state, but it should follow the project's existing narrative-first rules. The player should not operate a movement-management spreadsheet.

Store and reveal concrete assets such as:

- named witnesses and their willingness to speak;
- documents, recordings, datasets and chains of evidence;
- legal standing and available lawyers;
- volunteers, local organisers, stewards and venues;
- funds, transport and practical capacity;
- journalists and trusted public messengers;
- sympathetic councillors, MPs, officials, union officers or institutional insiders;
- a workplace or market choke point;
- polling concentration or a constituency where opposition coordination is possible;
- a draft policy, amendment, court remedy, procurement standard or administrative order;
- rival frames, hostile coverage, movement fatigue and internal fracture;
- credible successors who can continue without the headline hero.

The player experiences these as people, places, invitations, documents and remembered obligations. The narrative controller can reason over them without exposing a generic “movement power” meter.

# 10. Political opportunity as authored-emergent structure

The heroes should not simply grind until a support threshold unlocks the final quest. Nor should the national crisis be a fixed cutscene that makes earlier conduct irrelevant.

The game should contain several possible opportunity windows:

- a by-election after a resignation or scandal;
- a governing-party split;
- a court disclosure;
- an election campaign in which a concentrated constituency becomes pivotal;
- a budget or legislative deadline;
- a catastrophic service failure;
- a leaked official report;
- an inquiry finding;
- a strike or contract renewal;
- an opposition stand-down or unexpected elite endorsement;
- a cultural moment that changes the public interpretation of an old case.

The exact window that becomes usable depends on what the movement has accumulated. A by-election is useless without a candidate, local organisation and a plausible coalition. A policy meeting is useless without a drafted remedy. A legal opening is useless without standing and evidence. A mass demonstration is useless if nobody has identified the decision it is meant to force.

The heroes can also help create opportunities. Tabitha can make an ignored issue publicly urgent. Lord Pothole can turn an otherwise forgettable election into a national symbolic event. Gary can prepare a programme and cultivate insiders so that a scandal has somewhere politically useful to go.

# 11. The actual fighting chance

The trio becomes capable of a national victory when it possesses four things simultaneously:

1. **A real leverage mechanism** — electoral danger, legal authority, industrial pressure, administrative failure, institutional reputation or control of a necessary coalition.
2. **An executable decision** — somebody can sign, vote, order, judge, contract, fund or implement something specific.
3. **A political opening** — the normal defenders of the status quo are divided, discredited, constrained or newly dependent on the movement.
4. **Implementation capacity** — allies and institutions exist to turn the announcement into reality and defend it afterward.

Popularity, moral righteousness and viral visibility help, but none is a substitute for those four.

This gives the heroes a genuine chance without making the country implausibly easy to rescue.

# 12. The national story structure

The research supports a larger campaign with the following dramatic architecture.

## I. The impossible coalition

A specific failure brings the three together. They collaborate for incompatible reasons and win something small but real. The victory demonstrates that their powers combine unusually well.

## II. The pattern

Several local adventures appear unrelated. Gary begins connecting them. Tabitha finds that the same social constituency keeps appearing. Lord Pothole discovers that the same institutional evasions can be turned into a repeatable public language.

## III. Audience becomes organisation

Their fame grows, but the story distinguishes followers from useful capacity. Venues, chapters, witnesses, money, expert allies, legal support and local organisers appear. The player can strengthen, distort or exploit this conversion.

## IV. The first leverage victory

They do not merely attract attention. They force an authority to do something it had refused to do: lose a court case, reverse a decision, settle a strike, concede a vote, preserve a service or accept a binding standard.

The victory changes how everybody interprets them.

## V. Capture and counter-mobilisation

Parties, media figures, donors, institutions and hostile movements attempt to adopt, split, brand, criminalise or discredit them. The trio's public images diverge from their actual work.

## VI. Authentic fracture

Their source-shaped strategies become incompatible.

- Lord Pothole sees an electoral opening.
- Tabitha sees a moment for mass action and refuses elite containment.
- Gary sees a narrow policy route and regards both public performances as dangerous distractions.

Romance can intensify the fracture but cannot be its only cause.

## VII. The window

A crisis, scandal, election, judgment or party split makes national change possible. The opening is temporary.

The player may coordinate all three pathways, choose one, sabotage one to save another, leak strategy, give the state advance warning, become campaign manager, broker an insider agreement or stay outside the adventure.

## VIII. Breakthrough

The heroes can win something historically meaningful: a national settlement, institutional reconstruction, major policy adoption, legal remedy, constitutional transfer or durable insurgent foothold.

The precise substance should be designed only after choosing the main national issue threads. The strongest umbrella question emerging from these three parodies is:

> **Who owns Britain, who is Britain for, and who gets to decide?**

Gary answers through wealth and ownership. Tabitha answers through nation, belonging and participation. Lord Pothole answers through civic institutions and the ordinary public realm. Their answers overlap enough to form a movement and conflict enough to sustain a game.

## IX. Making it stick

The final act is implementation, coalition maintenance, succession and defence against reversal. The victory remains real even if Britain is not perfected.

Lord Pothole must govern without becoming normal. Gary must share authority with specialists and successors. Tabitha must accept responsibility for the people gathered under her symbol and decide whether the country she defends can contain people she did not originally imagine inside it.

# 13. Player freedom inside the epic story

The player should never be declared the chosen leader by default.

## Joining

When the player accepts an expedition, it becomes a genuinely shared activity. The group leaves together, waits, argues, responds to the same obstacle and has reasons for each person to act. This directly addresses the failed feeling of chasing Tabitha as she performs a prewritten life.

## Romance

Romance changes strategic trust, disclosure, public legitimacy, coalition dynamics and the private cost of campaigning. It does not sit in a separate dating mode.

Dating multiple heroes can work because each relationship has authored boundaries. Open negotiation, political convenience, emotional ambiguity and deception produce different consequences. The movement may publicly interpret the same arrangement differently from the people inside it.

## Sabotage

Sabotage remains plausible rather than labelled. The player can withhold evidence, strengthen a rival frame, expose private strategy, provoke a premature action, support an institution's legally defensible position, take credit or make one hero dependent on the player.

Some sabotage can be morally reasonable because the heroes can be wrong.

## Civilian life

Refusing the adventure remains a complete perspective. The campaign proceeds imperfectly without the player. The civilian route experiences consequences through work, rent, relationships, public spaces, media, local shortages, arguments and people returning from the movement.

A player who stayed outside may later become decisive precisely because they retain a relationship, occupation or institutional access the heroic coalition neglected.

# 14. Dramatic poses and visual identity

The *Little Nightmares II* reference should inform staging grammar, not be copied as a pose sheet.

Each silhouette should express political method:

- **Lord Pothole:** vertical, ceremonial, apparently immovable; the figure who declares a place historically important.
- **Tabitha:** forward, compressed and ready to move; the figure who will climb down into the situation and bring others with her.
- **Gary:** braced against or pointing into the structure; the figure who appears to see the mechanism underneath the tableau.

The strongest compositions place them on failed but recognisably British civic objects: a final bus, temporary bridge, broken flood barrier, condemned hall, scaffolding, traffic island or dismantled regeneration display.

Use the heroic tableau at departures, chapter openings, confrontations and breakthroughs. Follow it with the private aftermath: cold hands, wet coats, logistics, doubt, attraction and argument. The contrast allows the pose to be sincere rather than merely ironic.

# 15. Failure modes the campaign must model

The historical cases suggest at least these recurrent failures:

- viral attention without organisation;
- huge turnout without leverage;
- a correct diagnosis without an executable remedy;
- a leader who becomes the movement's bottleneck;
- co-option that preserves the brand while removing the demand;
- credibility scandal;
- tactical escalation that destroys coalition breadth;
- legal or administrative victory without implementation;
- a policy win that is later reversed;
- internal fracture over strategy, identity, money or credit;
- burnout and succession failure;
- a public symbol becoming detached from the actual person;
- satire becoming a safe entertainment product for the institutions it was meant to threaten.

These are not merely loss states. They are dramatic material and can produce partial victories, compromised endings and later recovery.

# 16. What is now settled and what is not

## Strong design conclusions from the research

- Lord Pothole, Tabitha and the Gary parody can carry the game as its headline heroic trio.
- Their ambition to save Britain should be treated as sincere and potentially achievable.
- Local stories are campaign-building episodes, not the final scale of the premise.
- Each character needs non-negotiable parody invariants and an original invention zone.
- Their powers are complementary: framing, mobilisation and structural programme.
- Success must pass through organisation, leverage, a decision vehicle, political opportunity and implementation.
- The player can join, love, betray, organise or ignore the movement without becoming its automatic leader.
- The final act should be about making a historic victory real and durable, not merely defeating a villain.

## Still deliberately unresolved

- The exact national settlement they pursue.
- Which two or three issue chains provide the campaign's main spine.
- The precise fictional biography and final names/designs of Lord Pothole and the Gary parody.
- The institutional form of the breakthrough: election, legislation, court, settlement, party adoption or a combination.
- The first full authored expedition to prototype under this new model.

Those questions should now be answered through scenario design rather than more abstract speculation. We have enough historical mechanism to stop treating success as magic.

# 17. Legal and ethical production constraint

“Unmistakable parody” and “safe commercial use” are related but not identical goals.

UK law contains a fair-dealing exception for caricature, parody and pastiche, but the assessment is fact-specific and does not provide an unrestricted licence to reproduce protected expression. Count Binface is itself an authored character; Amelia belongs to an authored educational work; Gary Stevenson and Jon Harvey are real people. False factual allegations, copied artwork/dialogue, confusing promotional use and implied endorsement create separate risks.

The production rule should therefore be:

- make the targets recognisable through premise, method, rhythm and satirical transformation;
- keep names, designs, dialogue, scenarios and invented biography original;
- clearly fictionalise disputed real-world claims rather than presenting them as verified facts;
- do not use voice cloning or copied visual assets;
- commission a UK IP and defamation review before commercial release and before marketing explicitly identifies the parody targets.

This is a production constraint, not a reason to weaken the satire.
