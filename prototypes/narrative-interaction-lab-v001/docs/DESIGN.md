# Design specification

## Creative hierarchy

The prototypes assume this priority order:

1. Authored characters, relationships and dramatic quality.
2. Inhabiting a particular life in contemporary Britain.
3. Player expression and consequential participation.
4. Physical, temporal and material embodiment.
5. Narrative personalisation and pacing systems.
6. Strategic or mechanical depth.

A mechanism fails when it improves a lower priority by materially damaging a higher one.

## Core hypothesis

The working hypothesis is not that *Managed Decline* needs an independent toy equivalent to combat.

It is:

> A consistent narrative interaction language can make authored social narrative feel inhabited, expressive, legible, consequential and memorable without competing with the narrative for attention.

The recurring support grammar is:

```text
be present or absent
→ notice and interpret
→ speak, act, withhold or leave
→ commit or withdraw
→ create a small physical/social consequence
→ retain visible residue
```

## Abstraction gaps represented in v001

### Told but not witnessed

An event may be described after the fact rather than physically experienced.

### Chosen but not performed

A dialogue label such as “comfort her” may resolve too much meaning in one click.

### Changed but not visible

Relationship and institutional state may change only in hidden data.

### Earned but not possessed

Access, trust or intimacy may have no object, ritual, place or behaviour demonstrating them.

### Important but not remembered

A significant scene may leave no callback, object, altered routine or environmental trace.

### Physical but meaningless

Walking and clicking may add friction without changing attention, expression or consequence.

## Narrative-support families

### Presence

Approach, remain, leave, accompany, wait, stand with, sit beside, follow.

### Expression

Speak, joke, reassure, challenge, disclose, conceal, redirect, remain silent, act instead of speaking.

### Commitment

Accept, tentatively agree, refuse, cancel, arrive late, renegotiate, bring somebody else.

### Interpretation

Observe, compare accounts, ask grounded questions, preserve uncertainty, circulate a provisional account.

### Material ritual

Bring, give, keep, return, save, delete, forward, photograph, wear, place.

### Residue

Object, photograph, message history, changed place, routine, access, public record, remembered conduct.

### Rhythm

Linger, interrupt, montage, afterglow, silence, compress routine.

## Quality gate

A production candidate should pass every relevant requirement.

### Narrative necessity

Removing it loses a specific emotional, causal, expressive or thematic quality.

### Semantic clarity

The player understands the action in ordinary human terms.

### Foreseeable distinction

The player can anticipate how alternatives differ in meaning without being promised an exact result.

### Believability

The action and reaction fit the current people, knowledge, relationship and context.

### Consequence

It changes interpretation, behaviour, information flow, commitment, access, physical state, future scene context or persistent memory.

### Visible shadow

The consequence becomes inferable without requiring an exposed score.

### Narrative proportionality

The interaction is no longer or more complex than its dramatic purpose warrants.

### Low learning burden

Recurring interactions are consistent; bespoke landmark interactions are understood immediately.

### No optimisation capture

The interface does not make one strategy obviously superior to role-playing.

### No maintenance debt

One meaningful act does not create routine upkeep unless upkeep is the dramatic subject.

### Tonal consistency

Input, timing, audiovisual treatment and consequence support the scene’s emotional register.

### Production proportionality

The interaction is reusable or the landmark scene is important enough to justify bespoke implementation.

### Accessibility

Meaning is not dependent solely on dexterity, speed, hearing, colour perception or inaccessible physical input.

## Variant hypotheses

### Dialogue baseline

Tests whether the narrative already works without extra interaction. The baseline is allowed to win.

### Narrative support

Tests whether small embodied choices make the player retell the scene through first-person conduct rather than only summarising dialogue.

### System-forward stress test

Tests the opposing hypothesis that explicit goals, meters and optimisation improve clarity and agency. It also reveals thematic costs:

- people becoming resources;
- intimacy becoming social combat;
- interpretation becoming a correctness puzzle;
- events becoming gauges to balance.

## Continue evidence

Continue developing a support mechanism when players:

- describe what they did in first-person physical/social terms;
- correctly understand character motives and causal relationships;
- believe their conduct altered the meaning of the scene;
- remember a place, object, gesture or silence;
- expect visible aftermath;
- do not treat the interaction as a chore or minigame;
- produce meaningfully different experiences from the same authored material.

## Kill or simplify evidence

Remove or reduce a mechanism when:

- players focus on performing it correctly instead of attending to the scene;
- an optimal strategy overrides expression;
- the interaction delays but does not change meaning;
- it needs disproportionate tutorialisation;
- it repeats visibly after a small number of uses;
- players remember the interface more than the characters for the wrong reason;
- the support version underperforms the dialogue baseline.
