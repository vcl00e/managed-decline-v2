# Playtest protocol

## Recommended session

Target one uninterrupted run plus immediate debrief.

Expected total session time: approximately **40–55 minutes** including the interview. The interactive evening itself is intended to land in the **35–45 minute** region, but actual duration is an empirical result rather than a requirement to force.

## Before play

Tell the participant only:

> This is a rough narrative prototype. Play it as yourself or as the version of the protagonist you find interesting. There is no designated main quest and no correct ending. Think aloud only if you normally can without disrupting your experience.

Do **not** explain:

- the three arrangement model;
- hidden commitments;
- the intended trade-offs;
- the four endings;
- what observations unlock;
- the key's mechanical role;
- the design success criteria.

Use normal mode, not `?annotate=1`.

## During play

Observe without coaching.

Record especially:

- first location and why they chose it;
- whether they inspect the hall slip or pub invoice;
- whether they create concrete commitments;
- moments of hesitation longer than ordinary reading;
- whether they refer to people/relationships or to "the game" when reasoning;
- whether they remember the last-bus constraint without reminders;
- whether they voluntarily revise a previous intention after the pub becomes socially real;
- whether the optional evidence creates an unsupported action they want to take;
- whether they consider leaving legitimate;
- whether the final material act feels overdetermined or expressive.

Do not rescue a participant who chooses to ignore a thread. Ignoring should be testable behaviour.

## Immediate debrief

Use the in-prototype ratings first, before discussion.

The built-in interview prompts cover the decisive questions. Follow up with concrete reconstruction rather than abstract preference questions.

Useful prompts:

- "At 18:00, what did you think your evening was about?"
- "When did that change?"
- "What did Maya expect from you? What did Tabitha expect?"
- "What would have happened if you simply left earlier?"
- "What exactly did the key let you do, and what did it *not* let you do?"
- "What did you think Sophie was trying to protect?"
- "Which outcome felt most tempting before the final choice? Why?"
- "What do you expect these people to remember tomorrow?"

## Core measures

Built-in 1–5 ratings:

- presence;
- agency;
- comprehension;
- pull;
- burden;
- map usefulness;
- dialogue fatigue.

Treat ratings as supporting evidence. The participant's causal retelling is more important.

## Strong evidence for the hypothesis

A participant spontaneously says something structurally like:

> "I wanted to keep my promise to Tabitha, but by then I'd also told Maya I would report back, and after seeing the pub terms I didn't want Sophie to use the photo as proof nothing had changed. So I tried to make the event smaller instead of solving everything."

The wording need not resemble this. What matters is that the player model contains **people, arrangements, incompatible terms, revised intent and consequence**.

## Strong evidence against the hypothesis

Examples:

- the player optimises for ending categories without caring about the people;
- they cannot explain why earlier commitments affected later decisions;
- practical possibilities feel unrelated to relationships;
- the key reads as a simple item-gate;
- the public/private information choices feel like an evidence score;
- the convergence is exhausting exposition rather than a situation to inhabit;
- the morning aftermath fails to create any future-oriented desire;
- the player repeatedly asks what they are "supposed" to do.

## Trace handling

Use **Export traces** after the session.

Store supplied raw JSON unchanged in this prototype's `findings/` directory when recording formal findings. Keep it readable; do not compress it.

A substantial test/feedback conversation should produce the next numbered findings file under this prototype version.
