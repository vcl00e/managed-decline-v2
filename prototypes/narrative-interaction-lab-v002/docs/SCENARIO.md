# Scenario specification — Listening Exercise

## Canonical status

This is a **research fixture**, not an accepted *Managed Decline* storyline. Its names, institutions, relationships and dialogue may be discarded after testing. What matters is the interaction evidence.

## Premise

On a wet Friday evening in Greystone, Maya’s community-radio programme is displaced from Barlow Rooms by a late “temporary electrical access restriction.” The room is nevertheless powered for the council’s next-day **GREYSTONE LISTENS** pilot launch. The radio group relocates to The Lantern pub and broadcasts anyway.

The player has simultaneously:

- invited Tabitha for reasons that may be personal, strategic or mixed;
- asked Sophie to attend without giving her a reliable plan;
- arrived before the official explanation;
- become a possible witness to the hall contradiction;
- encountered a photograph that is useful, unsafe and easy to misread.

No character assigns a quest.

## Identity envelope

### Premise invariants

- an existing community activity is displaced by a risk/administrative distinction;
- an official listening initiative occupies the same visibly powered place;
- the displaced activity still happens in altered form;
- public evidence is morally and factually complicated;
- the player’s intimate and public motives overlap without becoming identical.

### Dramatic invariants

- person versus public usefulness;
- continuation versus institutional recognition;
- private leverage versus public pressure;
- safety procedure versus selective administrative capacity;
- an event preserving its identity after being forced into the wrong room;
- no participant possessing the whole truth or a clean solution.

### Iconic elements

- the wet square with all three venues visible;
- the chained hall door;
- GREYSTONE LISTENS banners in a lit room;
- LOW SIGNAL equipment under a STORE / DISPOSE label;
- a council flyer folded until only LIST remains;
- the final song receiving applause in the wrong room;
- Saturday returning to the same geography.

### Mechanical identity

- choose whom to approach first;
- triangulate official wording, operational fact and personal motive;
- decide whether to become a public witness;
- choose information flow without a correctness meter;
- leave residue in place, message history, routine and relationship memory.

### Thematic question

> When an institution can recognise “community” only through an approved programme, what happens to the people already doing the thing?

## Locations

### Station Square

The compact social geography. Café, hall and pub remain visible from one another. Crossing the square changes who appears together and who can witness the association.

### Platform One Café

The intimate threshold. Tabitha waits at a table for two while looking at the council banners. The scene asks whether she has been invited as a person, an event or both.

### Barlow Rooms

The institutional contradiction. The public can see that the room is powered, but access depends on paperwork, role and approved use rather than electricity alone.

### The Lantern

The wrong room that becomes a possible new social place. The broadcast should contain genuine pleasure and ordinary life rather than functioning only as a protest meeting.

### Saturday morning

The same map carries changed wording, equipment disposition, messages and possible future access. Progression appears as a life becoming different, not an unlock banner.

## Cast

### Maya

- **Role:** Low Signal organiser and host.
- **Immediate aim:** keep the evening alive without letting relocation become proof that community use was protected.
- **Risk:** every act of resistance can make the programme about the council rather than the work she wanted to share.
- **Knowledge:** received the restriction email; knows the group’s booking history; does not know the full council property strategy.
**Voice:** quick institutional parody, followed by precise statements of what she actually needs.

### Tabitha

- **Role:** intimate thread and socially consequential presence.
- **Immediate aim:** determine whether the player wants her or wants what her presence does to a room.
- **Risk:** either speaking or remaining silent becomes a public performance interpreted by others.
- **Knowledge:** understands symbolic use; lacks operational hall facts at arrival.
**Voice:** compressed, exact, allergic to moral euphemism; jokes by editing other people’s framing.

### Sophie

- **Role:** friend, social cost and outside interpreter.
- **Immediate aim:** understand what she was invited to and avoid becoming unpaid emergency staff without acknowledgement.
- **Risk:** her competence makes other people assume consent.
- **Knowledge:** has the public website, messages and visible square; no insider access.
**Voice:** dry inventory of incompatible expectations, affectionate only after naming the inconvenience.

### Theo

- **Role:** photographer and evidence problem.
- **Immediate aim:** use a visually powerful image honestly.
- **Risk:** safe cropping weakens context; full publication exposes private details and makes Len look culpable.
- **Knowledge:** possesses the image, not the institutional truth.
**Voice:** visually enthusiastic, then self-correcting when aesthetic excitement collides with consequence.

### Nadia Okafor

- **Role:** council Community Partnership Delivery officer.
- **Immediate aim:** preserve a defensible public account while preventing a temporary restriction from turning into an empty or closed building.
- **Risk:** the only language available to her makes existing groups disappear grammatically before they disappear physically.
- **Knowledge:** knows the consolidation direction and funded-operator possibility; does not control Estates or possess a complete electrical assessment.
**Voice:** professional passive constructions under pressure, more direct in private, capable of recognising absurdity without being able to remove it.

### Len

- **Role:** caretaker and operational witness.
- **Immediate aim:** follow the restriction, keep the building safe and avoid being turned into the face of a decision he did not make.
- **Risk:** he physically handles keys and boxes, so photographs can imply authorship.
- **Knowledge:** knows what is powered, requested, delivered and actually happening in the room.
**Voice:** concrete nouns and procedural facts; little interest in policy framing.

## Hidden situation state

### Actual state

- Estates asked whether evening groups had current room-specific risk reviews.
- Most existing groups do not because this was not previously required.
- An electrician has been requested but not booked.
- The listening-pilot contractor supplied a risk pack and equipment.
- The pilot equipment is temporarily powered through an extension lead.
- The council is considering a funded operator/consolidated evening-booking arrangement.
- No formal decision has terminated Low Signal’s future use.
- Future bookings are not being confirmed, which may create displacement without a single closure decision.

### Public state

- Barlow Rooms is described as operating normally.
- Evening access is described as temporarily restricted while the electrical position is clarified.
- The listening pilot is described as a separately controlled activation.
- No community provision is officially described as affected.

### Belief state

- Maya suspects the restriction is a route to removing existing groups.
- Nadia believes a funded operator may be the only realistic route to keeping the hall active.
- Sophie believes everyone is obtaining something from the crisis except Len.
- Tabitha believes all available positions will be interpreted, including silence.
- Theo initially believes the photograph is perfect evidence, then recognises its distortions.

### Rumour/public-narrative possibilities

- “Council listening event silences radio group.”
- “The hall was closed for electrical safety.”
- “The radio group was consulted and successfully relocated.”
- “The council hates music.”
- “A funded operator saved the building.”

None is a complete account.

## Affordance and residue outputs

Possible consequences include:

- The Lantern offering another Thursday.
- Maya possessing evidence for later use.
- Nadia receiving an accountable institutional record.
- A public post continuing beyond the player’s control.
- The official account remaining cleaner because the image stayed private.
- Low Signal equipment being held rather than moved toward disposal.
- A public memory of arriving with Tabitha.
- A relationship conversation remaining open without becoming a score.
- Exact institutional wording becoming player knowledge.

The scenario does not announce these as unlocks. They should appear as messages, behaviour, place use and future eligibility.
