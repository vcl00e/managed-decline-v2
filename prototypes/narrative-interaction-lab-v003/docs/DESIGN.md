# Design specification

## Why v003 exists

`narrative-interaction-lab-v002` failed almost immediately in play. The tester stopped after approximately two minutes and described it as boring, not funny and lacking a clear emotional journey.

The accepted diagnosis is that v002 began from a coherent causal model and hoped entertainment would emerge from it. It tested arrangements, commitments, information and residue before proving that the content itself was worth inhabiting.

V003 reverses that hierarchy.

## Research question

> Can a tightly authored 8–12 minute *Managed Decline* slice create immediate comic pull, rapid desire for a character, meaningful emotional movement, escalation and a satisfying social choice — while keeping the underlying consequence machinery quiet?

The experiment should be considered a failure if the tester again wants to stop around minute two, regardless of whether later material is structurally good.

## Player fantasy

The player is not a civic fixer.

The immediate fantasy is:

> **You have been privately invited into the orbit of a charismatic, politically troublesome young woman and become her co-conspirator while an institution accidentally explains her to herself.**

That fantasy contains:

- privileged access;
- comic complicity;
- possible attraction;
- social danger / embarrassment;
- the pleasure of seeing public institutional language collide with an actual person;
- an opportunity to demonstrate whether the player treats Tabitha as a person or another spectacle.

## Premise

Tabitha Mercer became notorious after a painfully earnest public-sector anti-extremism / online-vulnerability training scenario turned her into a simplified case study. The public found her more charismatic than the programme intended.

Years later, Bellwether Borough Council runs a “legacy learning” session using a refreshed edition of the programme.

Tabitha asks the player to attend with her without revealing her identity to the facilitator.

The joke is legible immediately:

```text
PROJECTOR:
WHAT WOULD YOU DO IF YOUR FRIEND TABITHA
BEGAN EXPRESSING CHALLENGING VIEWS ONLINE?

ACTUAL TABITHA, BESIDE PLAYER:
Still no option D: ask Tabitha out. Cowards.
```

No warm-up scene precedes this.

## Emotional journey

### 1. Surprise — first seconds

The player immediately understands the comic contradiction: fictional Tabitha is being analysed on screen while actual Tabitha sits beside them.

Target feeling:

> What the hell is this? This is funny.

### 2. Complicity / attraction

Tabitha asks the player not to identify her yet. The player becomes part of the joke rather than merely observing it.

Target feeling:

> I am in on this with her.

### 3. Delight / escalation

The workshop discusses warning signs while accidentally validating the caricature: purple hair, British history, irony and so on.

Target feeling:

> I want to hear the next thing this institution says in front of her.

### 4. Personal sting

The refreshed ending shows a smiling, volunteering Tabitha with a first-person redemption quote she never gave.

The important shift is not “institutional facts become more complex.” It is:

> The joke has done something to a person I have begun enjoying.

Tabitha's line:

> “The old one thought I was dangerous. Fine. At least it disliked something I actually said. This one says I got better.”

Target feeling:

> Oh. This actually hurts her.

### 5. Oh-shit exposure

The workshop tries to connect its booked “lived experience contributor” remotely. Tabitha's phone rings in the room.

Target feeling:

> This is about to go catastrophically public.

### 6. Meaningful social choice

The player does not choose a moral answer. They choose what kind of relationship to the moment and to Tabitha they want:

- support her taking the room publicly;
- prioritise her right not to become content and leave;
- make the institution explain its own language before she reveals herself.

### 7. Release and future pull

Every ending resolves the immediate emotional problem while opening a personal future with Tabitha.

The player should not feel “I completed the Tabitha scenario.”

Target feeling:

> I want another scene with her.

## Comedy requirements

The comedy should come from three sources.

### Precise institutional absurdity

Examples:

- the real Tabitha sitting through a scenario about herself;
- a facilitator carefully explaining that purple hair is not an extremism indicator while purple-haired cartoon Tabitha fills the screen;
- an invented first-person redemption quote labelled “participant voice synthesised from facilitated feedback”;
- the remote guest system trying to call the person physically sitting in the room.

The narration should not tell the player that these things are satire.

### Character comedy

Tabitha should generate jokes because of how she sees the world, not because she has been assigned “witty lines.” Her humour is a defence, a social invitation and sometimes an evasion.

Gareth is not a cartoon villain. His earnest professionalism makes the situation funnier because he is trying to behave correctly inside a fundamentally ridiculous setup.

### Callback / payoff

“Option D: ask Tabitha out” begins as a joke and can return as romantic/comic payoff at the end.

## Interaction model

Dialogue choices remain primary because v001 found that high-semantic dialogue choices were stronger than decomposing emotional intentions into gesture chains.

Only one or two physical acts are exposed when their form carries social meaning, such as covering Tabitha's ringing phone.

No map, inventory, goals, meters, calendar or plan canvas are present.

## Hidden causal state

V003 still records limited state:

- whether the player flirted;
- whether they offered escape;
- whether they tried to understand why Tabitha cares;
- whether they noticed / verified the fabricated quote;
- whether they protected her phone or deferred to her agency;
- which final social position they chose;
- a compact relationship interpretation and memories.

This state exists to preserve and inspect meaning. It is not the subject of the scene.

## Hard constraints from v002

Do not:

- begin neutrally and offer several equally mild things to investigate;
- use rain / routine / bureaucracy as a substitute for a hook;
- make every character reasonable and low-temperature;
- use narration to explain that something is satirical;
- expose the design argument in prose;
- assume causal complexity equals emotional movement;
- assume ordinary arrangements are interesting simply because their terms collide;
- lengthen the slice if the first two minutes fail.

## Falsification criteria

Treat the prototype as failed if any of these dominate the feedback:

- “I was bored before it got going.”
- “I understood the joke but it wasn't actually funny.”
- “Tabitha felt like a writer delivering quips rather than somebody I wanted to know.”
- “The fabricated quote was an obvious attempt to make me feel something.”
- “I didn't care whether she revealed herself or left.”
- “The choices were just three flavours of supporting her.”
- “It ended and I felt done.”

The strongest positive result is:

> **I was disappointed there wasn't another scene.**
