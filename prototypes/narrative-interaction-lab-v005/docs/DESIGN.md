# V005 design specification — lived space before core-loop abstraction

## Why this prototype exists

The previous attempted activity-spine prototype made a category error. It represented a continuous environment as a set of location buttons and advanced a scripted dramatic beat whenever the player chose an action. A hidden work backlog was then added as pressure.

That implementation could not answer the intended question because it removed the phenomena under investigation:

- physical presence;
- anticipation created by seeing somebody move somewhere;
- voluntary sequencing;
- overhearing;
- interruption;
- lingering;
- walking away;
- material intervention;
- spatially missing one thing because the player was genuinely occupied by another.

V005 restores those phenomena and deliberately avoids comparing three treatments of identical writing.

## Repository constraints carried forward

### 1. Direct gameplay only when attention or agency matters

Routine exists underneath the scene but is not simulated for its own sake. The player promised to help Maya with an evening broadcast. The prototype does not turn that commitment into repeated setup chores.

### 2. Simulate commitments, not chores

The extension reel is exposed because whether the radio room has power later can change the player's available response to a social/institutional collision. It is not present to create a generic task loop.

### 3. Walking must earn its place

Movement should create:

- spatial memory;
- anticipation;
- voluntary sequencing;
- social geography;
- visible NPC behaviour;
- optional human situations;
- pacing between conversations;
- materially different presence and audience.

If it only connects dialogue scenes, remove it.

### 4. Put cognitive work inside the fiction

The player should reason about people, claims, priorities and consequences. They should not need to learn a complicated control surface or plan through an exposed activity system.

### 5. Dialogue remains high-bandwidth

V001/v003 evidence still supports authored dialogue choices for semantically rich social actions. V005 therefore does not decompose every social intention into tiny physical gestures.

Physical action is exposed only when the fact of physically doing it matters: carrying power to the studio, being near an overheard conversation, reading a dropped document while it is available, opening a side door, being present with one person rather than another.

## Research question

> **Can a small continuous social space make the player form and pursue meaningful intentions that a dialogue-only presentation would not support as naturally?**

Secondary questions:

1. What activities does the player voluntarily perform?
2. What makes them leave one person or place for another?
3. Do they treat missed events as authored personal history or as lost content?
4. Which physical actions become socially meaningful?
5. Does the player exhibit systematic completion behaviour because the game looks like an RPG?
6. Does real-time continuity create useful pressure or simply punish reading/exploration?
7. What should remain continuous and what should compress in a production version?

## Scenario structure

### Starting arrangement

The player has told Maya they will help with Radio Bellwether's Thursday broadcast.

This is a commitment, not an objective card.

### Existing world situation

Bellwether Council is attempting to place the community rooms into a “meanwhile activation” arrangement with LocalityWorks. LocalityWorks has been supplied paperwork that assumes vacant possession within days. Current building users have not been clearly transitioned.

The situation exists independently of the player.

### Human arrangements inside it

- Maya expects to broadcast from the hall / radio room tonight.
- Ben expects valid bookings to continue until somebody with authority tells him otherwise.
- June expects her Saturday group to exist.
- Rowan expects to conduct a viewing of an operationally vacant asset.
- Priya expects to view a usable flexible workspace, not participate in a live displacement dispute.
- The player has promised to help Maya but has not promised how far they will involve themselves.

The collision is therefore not generated by “the game needs a dilemma”. It comes from incompatible expectations that are already simultaneously reasonable from each person's information.

## Spatial plan

The entire prototype is one compact building:

```text
┌──────────────┬──────────────────────────────┬───────────┐
│ FOYER        │                              │           │
│ front doors  │         MAIN HALL            │ courtyard │
├──────────────┤                              │           │
│ RADIO ROOM   │                              │           │
├──────────────┼──────────┬───────────────────┤           │
│ KITCHENETTE  │ STORE    │ BACK CORRIDOR     │           │
└──────────────┴──────────┴───────────────────┴───────────┘
```

Distances are intentionally compressed. Moving from one meaningful place to another should take seconds, not minutes.

## Continuous event grammar

Events fire according to world time and occur at particular places.

The player only receives the actual words when inside the hearing radius.

Important current sequence:

1. Rowan arrives for a “community asset familiarisation”.
2. Ben discovers Rowan's occupancy sheet calls the building vacant after seven.
3. June remarks that her Saturday booking apparently still exists.
4. Rowan takes a call in the courtyard and references vacant possession Monday.
5. Rowan drops his briefing pack in the foyer for a limited period.
6. Maya tests the broadcast; the result reflects whether the player physically powered the mixer.
7. Priya arrives expecting to view empty workspace.
8. The viewing and live broadcast become directly incompatible.

These events are intentionally perceivable in multiple ways:

- hear them directly;
- see characters move and follow them;
- miss them;
- learn parts later through conversation;
- inspect material residue such as the pack.

## No universal information requirement

There is no canonical evidence path the player must complete.

For example, learning about vacant possession Monday may happen by:

- following Rowan and overhearing his call;
- reading the dropped briefing pack;
- potentially inferring enough from other conversations without exact wording.

Missing the fact does not fail the scenario. It changes which actions are available and how the player interprets events.

## Material affordances

### Extension reel

State transition:

```text
store
→ carried by player
→ connected to radio mixer
→ radio room has reliable power
```

Narrative effect:

Maya can preserve the broadcast by retreating to the radio room, and a live-interview strategy is physically plausible.

The action is meaningful because of later context, not because carrying objects is intrinsically the game.

### Briefing pack

Exists temporarily after Rowan drops it.

Reading the visible page gives a concrete institutional claim:

> vacant possession target Monday

The player does not receive a generic “evidence item”. They know something and may choose whom to tell.

### Side door

June knows an old key still works. If the player learns this, the side entrance becomes usable.

During the collision, physically opening it can let arriving users into a hall somebody is attempting to make private for a viewing.

This is a strong test of whether world-state, character knowledge and physical action can form one narrative move.

## Social affordances

People are not information terminals. Conversation should expose character and strategy.

### Maya

Wants to do the show. She is willing to joke about the situation but does not automatically want to turn herself or the hall into protest content.

### Ben

Does not know the full property strategy. He strongly prefers written authority, valid bookings and not being personally made responsible for an undocumented closure.

### June

Has long institutional memory and casual practical access. Her old side-door key is exactly the kind of tiny accumulated relationship-with-place state a persistent game can make meaningful.

### Rowan

Not a cartoon villain. He has been sent to perform a legitimate professional task using bad / premature information. Once confronted with reality, he is forced to distinguish what he was told from what he actually knows.

### Priya

Is not “the developer”. She is a small-business owner who was told she was viewing vacant space and is uncomfortable becoming the human face of somebody else's property process.

## The clash is not initially an ending menu

At the collision, the player remains in the building.

Possible resolutions emerge from what they can physically reach and what they know:

- talk to Maya and preserve the show in the powered radio room;
- use exact knowledge with Maya to create a live interview;
- take the property assumption to Ben or Rowan and force an authority check;
- use June's key to open the side door to arriving users;
- do nothing long enough for an awkward compromise to emerge;
- leave the building at any earlier point.

This is still authored. The question is whether embedding authored possibilities in a continuous world changes the player's experience of choosing among them.

## Real-time policy

World time advances during free movement and pauses during dialogue.

Reason:

- events must continue while the player is elsewhere;
- reading speed should not be punished;
- the experiment is about attention through presence, not reflex pressure.

If even this softened real-time model produces completion anxiety, that is important evidence against using continuous timing heavily in production.

## Telemetry interpretation

Do not over-value outcome distribution.

The most useful traces are likely to be:

- where the player went immediately after gaining control;
- first voluntary interaction;
- whether they returned to Maya repeatedly;
- whether Rowan's movement caused pursuit;
- whether they spent time in rooms without explicit prompts;
- whether they found the pack naturally;
- what they were doing during missed ambient events;
- how often they changed direction after hearing something;
- whether they solved the radio power issue before understanding why it mattered;
- how long they remained after the clash resolved;
- whether they left before resolution.

## Falsification

V005 fails its main hypothesis if the player's positive experience can be cleanly reduced to “the dialogue was good” while movement and physicality add only friction.

It also fails if the only reliable way to make spatial play interesting is to create constant urgency, collectibles, chores, puzzle gates or explicit objectives.

A strong result is not “the player used every system.”

A strong result is:

> **The player tells a coherent story about what they decided to care about, why they went where they went, what they did, what they missed, and how being physically present changed the story they experienced.**
