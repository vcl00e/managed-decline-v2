# Narrative Interaction Lab v006 — Playtest record

Date: 2026-08-26

Status: **Recorded — two runs, with post-playtest clarification of ambiguous debrief measures**

## Test basis

V006 tested whether v003-style focused authored conversations and v005-style continuous lived space could operate as one causal experience inside the same compact scenario.

Two runs were completed and exported. The same scenario was replayed with materially different conduct:

- one run largely protected Tabitha's position, let Alex handle the disclosure, and left with Tabitha;
- one run took more responsibility for the viewing, declined to make the disclosure personally, and stayed at the flat.

The raw debrief answers are useful, but two questions were later found to conflate different constructs. The tester subsequently clarified what they meant. Those clarifications are part of this record and should override simplistic interpretation of the original form labels.

---

# Run 1 — diplomacy, delegation, leave with Tabitha

## Behavioural trace

The player:

- entered Tabitha's room and completed the initial focused conversation;
- chose the qualified promise to keep the situation quiet unless circumstances forced otherwise;
- remained elsewhere when Priya arrived, so Alex opened the front door;
- later chose to wave Priya into Tabitha's room before Alex's authored access beat;
- directly inspected the damp;
- photographed the damp;
- in the focused viewing conversation, chose to let Alex frame/explain the problem;
- finally chose to leave with Tabitha.

The run therefore combined:

- a stance-defining promise;
- one meaningful spatial access intervention;
- direct material observation;
- optional evidence creation;
- delegation of the substantive disclosure;
- and a final high-consequence positioning decision.

## Tester framing

The tester described their overall intention as largely going with the flow while preserving diplomacy, avoiding unnecessary upset, and not crossing their own boundaries.

They explicitly reasoned that Priya could decide whether to take the room and that Alex was the person most directly concerned with conducting the viewing. They also noted that the exact degree of Tabitha's responsibility would depend on prior arrangements between Tabitha and Alex, which the prototype did not establish clearly enough.

## Positive spatial evidence

Two spatial moments were singled out as things a dialogue-only treatment would flatten.

### Priya's arrival

When Priya's arrival was announced, the tester felt an immediate urge to move because the entrance was a real place in the environment and they knew somebody would appear there.

They later recognised that greeting Priya was not actually their responsibility, but the important point was that the arrival produced a genuine spatial/social reaction rather than an automatic scene transition.

### Entering Tabitha's room

The tester reported excitement at being invited into Tabitha's room. Physically crossing into the private space made the invitation feel more prominent than an automatic VN transition would have.

This supports treating social thresholds and access as one of the strongest uses of the diorama.

## Major negative evidence

The tester reported:

- large gaps where nothing seemed to be happening;
- walking around unable to interact with people or objects;
- events appearing to fire from an invisible timer;
- pacing feeling outside their control;
- a sense of wasting time between authored beats;
- pressure to search for something to do before the next timed sequence trapped them;
- and too many mapped areas whose gameplay significance was negligible.

They answered **Yes** to feeling pressure to poll for new content.

This is not merely a presentation complaint. The implementation exposed only a small number of contextual affordances while Alex and Priya could be visibly active yet socially unavailable.

---

# Run 2 — more independent stance, stay at the flat

## Behavioural trace

The player:

- spoke to Tabitha much earlier;
- explicitly refused to promise anything before seeing how events developed;
- answered the front door personally when Priya arrived;
- inspected and photographed the damp;
- let Alex determine bedroom access rather than opening it early;
- in the focused viewing conversation, chose the stance that the disclosure was not theirs to make;
- and finally chose to stay at the flat rather than leave with Tabitha.

This produced a useful contrast with Run 1 because the final positioning choice changed where the protagonist actually remained.

## Tester feedback

The same dead-time problem remained.

The tester described feeling ignored when Alex and Priya were present but could not be joined or addressed. Their movement around the flat became anticipatory searching for the next affordance rather than meaningful participation.

They also criticised the map for containing several rooms even though most meaningful play occurred around Tabitha's room and the damp.

Again, they answered **Yes** to polling pressure and only **Somewhat** to the original "world feedback" question.

# Debrief measurement corrections

Two original v006 questions were poorly specified and should not be used naively.

## 1. "Did the resumed world visibly reflect what you said?"

The tester initially answered **Somewhat**.

In discussion afterward, they did not understand exactly what was being measured. Once reframed, they distinguished two different effects:

### Expressive / interpretive effect

The choices meaningfully changed how the tester personally framed their role in the situation. They could decide whether they were a fixer, a bystander, somebody prioritising Priya's right to know, somebody deferring to Alex, or somebody simply staying close to Tabitha.

The tester subsequently rated this dimension around **4/5**.

This is positive evidence for expressive agency and self-definition.

### External situation divergence

The tester did **not** feel most dialogue choices changed what happened in the world very significantly.

The strongest exception was the final **leave with Tabitha / stay at the flat** choice, because it caused an immediate, obvious change in where the protagonist went and what they would experience next.

Therefore the original question conflated:

- self-definition / expressive agency;
- fictional acknowledgement;
- and actual situation divergence.

Future debriefs should measure these separately.

## 2. "Which moment needed the VN presentation rather than staying on the map?"

The tester initially could not identify one and answered that they did not remember.

This was not clean negative evidence about the VN treatment. The prototype had already moved nearly all important dialogue into focused VN presentation, so the question asked the tester to invent a counterfactual comparison that the build did not actually supply.

The tester later clarified that the important dialogue **did benefit from the VN presentation and that the focused treatment increased immersion**.

The unresolved question was not whether focused presentation had value. It was whether the game chose the correct thresholds for switching into it and whether the live spatial tissue around it was strong enough.

# Strongest individual choice

The tester identified the final decision — **leave with Tabitha / stay at the flat** — as the strongest choice in v006.

Why it worked:

- it was immediately human-readable;
- it changed physical presence;
- it expressed social priority;
- it acted on a concrete commitment/relationship context;
- it changed subsequent possibilities;
- and the consequence was legible without a meter or explanatory UI.

This is stronger evidence than the subtler map-state changes after the earlier viewing conversation.

The useful distinction is:

### Expressive choices

Define stance, interpretation, tone, or role. They may be worthwhile without large branching.

### Positioning / commitment choices

Change where the protagonist is, who they remain with, what they take responsibility for, or which arrangement they preserve. These can and often should produce larger, obvious divergence.

Managed Decline should support both rather than forcing every semantic dialogue option to carry the causal weight of a major commitment decision.

---

# Implementation defect affecting consequence evidence

Run 1 contained a contradiction in the final outcome summary.

The player chose to let Alex explain the damp. The state model correctly resolved the quiet commitment as **kept**, because the player delegated the disclosure rather than publicly exposing it themselves.

However, the final summary still stated that the promise to keep the evening quiet "did not survive contact with the viewing" because the outcome code treated any `dampDisclosed` state as a broken promise regardless of who disclosed it.

The departure dialogue used the same faulty assumption.

Therefore the original "world feedback" measure is partially contaminated by incorrect consequence reporting and must not be treated as clean evidence against persistent consequence in principle.

# Comparative assessment against v006 success criteria

| Criterion | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Spatial/world moment loses meaning as dialogue-only | **Positive** — Priya's arrival and entering Tabitha's private room |
| Important conversation benefits from focused VN presentation | **Positive after clarification** — focused treatment increased immersion |
| Spatial/material decision changes later drama | **Implemented, but only partly salient** |
| Dialogue choice changes resumed situation | **Weak for most choices; strong for final stay/leave positioning choice** |
| Player forms own stance/intention | **Positive** — diplomacy, boundaries, delegation, non-intervention |
| Player understands what they are preserving | **Partial** — stance was clear, but prior Tabitha/Alex responsibility was under-specified |
| Little/no polling pressure | **Failure** — both runs reported polling pressure |
| Timed world creates presence rather than arbitrary anxiety | **Mixed but overall failure** — Priya's arrival worked; the master schedule produced dead-time and pacing anxiety |
| Map worth retaining | **Positive but not yet campaign-scale proof** — specific spatial moments clearly benefited, but much of the map was semantically empty |

# Primary diagnosis

V006 did not demonstrate that the hybrid should be abandoned.

It demonstrated that two useful modes were connected by the wrong temporal and interaction grammar.

The strongest formulation is:

> **Continuous place showed value; continuous fixed-rate time did not.**

The map created value when:

- a threshold carried social meaning;
- somebody arrived somewhere the player understood spatially;
- the player chose where to be;
- or a final presence/absence decision visibly changed the situation.

The map failed when:

- the player had to burn seconds until a global timestamp;
- NPCs were visibly active but socially impermeable;
- rooms existed without distinct social functions;
- and the player searched for refreshed content because nothing signalled meaningful participation.

The corrective hypothesis for v006b is therefore:

> **Keep continuous geography, but use elastic diegetic time and make live social situations participable through positioning rather than NPC polling.**
