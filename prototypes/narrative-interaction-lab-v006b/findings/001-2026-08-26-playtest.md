# Playtest — Narrative Interaction Lab v006b

Date: 2026-08-26

Status: **Two completed comparison runs — strong evidence that the v006 pacing/polling failure was corrected**

## Purpose of v006b

V006b kept the same basic *The Viewing* scenario but replaced v006's fixed master timeline with:

- causal phases;
- elastic diegetic time;
- live-situation participation;
- meaningful spatial positioning;
- ambient map dialogue;
- and focused VN presentation only when a conversation warranted higher semantic bandwidth.

The key question was whether those changes could preserve the useful spatial effects found in v006 while removing dead time, NPC polling and the feeling that the script was invisibly waiting for timestamps.

## Run-level summary

Two completed runs produced deliberately different social conduct.

### Run 1 — stay with Tabitha / avoid taking over the viewing

The player:

- chose `tabitha_here_for_you`;
- stayed with Tabitha while Alex answered the door;
- deliberately chose to stay with Tabitha when Alex and Priya began the viewing;
- let Alex handle bedroom access;
- inspected and photographed the damp after the viewing reached the room;
- did **not** enter the focused viewing VN scene;
- let Alex answer Priya's damp question ambiently;
- and kept the pre-existing commitment to walk Tabitha to the station.

The tester reported:

- **waiting:** Never;
- **polling:** No;
- **meaningful positioning:** Yes;
- **natural participation / staying out:** Yes;
- **VN transitions:** fine;
- **attention:** the people and situation.

The strongest qualitative observation was that movement had begun to become narrative rather than merely navigational. The tester deliberately moved away from Alex and Priya so as not to become part of their conversation, and described physical positioning as a narrative choice.

The departure worked especially well. When Tabitha began leaving, the tester had to notice what was happening and catch her. They reported a small increase in heart rate and felt the moment would have lost meaning if it had been automatically authored rather than requiring real-time observation and response.

The preferred stance in this run was non-intervention. Staying with Tabitha and not becoming responsible for Alex/Priya's uncomfortable housing situation was itself enjoyable. The remaining weakness was that this route could feel somewhat idle because staying out of the viewing did not yet provide much positive companionship activity beyond remaining near Tabitha.

### Run 2 — join the viewing / intervene / stay at the flat

The player:

- chose `tabitha_priya`, prioritising Priya's right to know;
- allowed Alex to answer Priya's arrival by default;
- deliberately joined Alex and Priya as the viewing started;
- asked them to give Tabitha a minute before entering the bedroom;
- entered the focused VN room conversation;
- chose to let Alex explain the damp rather than disclosing it personally;
- and finally broke the pre-existing station-walk commitment by staying at the flat.

The tester again reported:

- **waiting:** Never;
- **polling:** No;
- **meaningful positioning:** Yes;
- **natural participation / staying out:** Yes;
- **VN transitions:** fine;
- **attention:** the people and situation.

The tester specifically valued deciding physically whether to open/access the room and whether to join the conversation. These decisions felt richer through movement and positioning than if they had existed only as dialogue options.

However, this run exposed the next major weakness. The tester felt they had behaved intrusively: they came to help Tabitha, inserted themselves into her housing situation, influenced the viewing, and then failed to help her to the station. They expected Tabitha to challenge, resent or otherwise react more strongly. Instead, the situation remained unusually accommodating and held together without meaningful escalation.

This is evidence that the interaction grammar improved enough for the **social interpretation / consequence model** to become the next visible bottleneck.

## Direct comparison with v006

V006's major failure signals were:

- long gaps where nothing meaningful could be done;
- a sense of waiting for invisible timers;
- pressure to poll people/objects for hidden content;
- NPC activity that could be observed but not naturally joined;
- and attention shifting toward operating the prototype rather than inhabiting the situation.

Across both v006b runs:

- waiting was reported as **Never**;
- polling was **No**;
- live situations could be joined or avoided;
- meaningful positioning was reported **Yes**;
- and the tester said their attention was on **the people and situation**.

This is strong directional evidence that v006b corrected the specific v006 interaction/time failure.

## Important design evidence

### 1. The diorama's strongest function is positioning, not exploration

The strongest spatial decisions were not about discovering map content. They were about deciding where to put the protagonist relative to an unfolding human situation:

- stay with Tabitha;
- join Alex and Priya;
- remain outside a conversation;
- approach a threshold;
- protect a room boundary;
- catch someone before they leave;
- leave with one person rather than remain with another situation.

Working principle:

> **Movement earns narrative value when physical position changes participation, audience, intimacy, responsibility or what the player can still do next.**

### 2. Time should be meaningful, not merely short

The tester spent substantial real time moving around before the first conversation in one run yet did not report waiting.

This suggests the distinction is not simply long versus short intervals.

> **Time feels dead when the player is forced to burn it until the script advances. Time can remain uncompressed when observation, presence, voluntary movement or potential action still have meaning.**

Elastic time should therefore compress genuinely empty intervals, not automatically remove all quiet time.

### 3. Opting out is a valid form of agency

The tester's favourite path in the first run was effectively to stay with Tabitha and refuse to become responsible for the viewing.

This supports a strong Managed Decline principle:

> **Interesting situations should not imply that the player is obliged to intervene. Choosing whose problem not to adopt can itself be meaningful play.**

However, non-intervention should still contain positive experience. Staying with a character should provide companionship, observation, small exchanges or other lived value rather than becoming mechanical inactivity.

### 4. VN focus and map play are now cooperating better

The tester reported that the VN transitions felt fine in both runs.

The useful division is increasingly clear:

- map / diorama: presence, thresholds, following, joining, avoiding, catching, observing, light ambient exchange;
- VN focus: concentrated high-semantic conversation and expressive choices.

The remaining question is no longer primarily whether those modes can coexist. It is whether larger arrangements and persistent character interpretation can make their consequences accumulate convincingly.

### 5. Compact topology improved focus

The simplified flat was reported as more focused and better at carrying the story.

At the same time, the tester noticed a loss of environmental texture and sense of how the flat was actually lived in.

This suggests:

> **Compact interactive topology; rich environmental/material texture.**

The solution is not necessarily more mechanically active rooms. Production environments can remain visually dense, autobiographical and lived-in without expanding the number of spaces the player must patrol for interaction.

## Remaining weaknesses

### Social interpretation is too forgiving

Run 2 produced a coherent pattern of potentially irritating conduct, but Tabitha did not interpret that pattern strongly enough.

Future character response should consider accumulated behaviour such as:

- what the player originally came to do;
- whether they repeatedly chose somebody else's problem;
- whether they inserted themselves into a private matter;
- whether they protected or exposed somebody;
- whether they fulfilled or broke an existing commitment;
- and whether their words and subsequent actions were consistent.

The desired result is not an exposed relationship score. It is believable character initiative and acknowledgement.

### Situation divergence remains limited

The tester felt able to express different stances and participate differently, but in the interventionist run they felt they were pushing the situation without being able to make much actually escalate or fail.

This should not be solved by forcing every semantic choice into a large branch. The next test should instead allow accumulated conduct, commitments and arrangement collisions to produce genuinely different practical/social states when causally warranted.

### Non-intervention needs positive content

Staying with Tabitha worked emotionally but could become idle. Future scenarios should support low-pressure companionship as a legitimate reward rather than making the player choose between intervention and inactivity.

## What v006b does not establish

V006b does **not** prove:

- that a full campaign-scale explorable world is worth its production cost;
- that several simultaneous arrangements can remain readable;
- that phone / asynchronous interaction works with this grammar;
- that the character model can interpret accumulated conduct;
- that long-term relationships will produce convincing initiative or conflict;
- that a larger set of locations will preserve this focus;
- or that *The Viewing* itself should become production content.

Both runs answered **Unsure** when asked whether removing explorable space entirely would make the experience worse. That should be preserved as a caveat even though the written feedback identified several specific spatial moments that would lose meaning without direct movement.

## Playtest status

**V006b successfully corrected the targeted v006 pacing/polling failure and produced the strongest evidence so far that spatial positioning can itself carry narrative agency.**

The next bottleneck is no longer basic map/VN integration. It is whether persistent arrangements and character interpretation can understand a pattern of conduct and generate the next meaningful situations.
