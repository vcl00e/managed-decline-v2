# V009 plain interaction script — Two Pictures

**Purpose:** judge the interaction and spoken language before code/state machinery exists.

## Situation

The community hall event is winding down. On a side table is a cheap instant camera. The counter says **2**.

Tabitha is already standing with the player. She picks up the camera, checks the counter, and raises it toward them.

**Tabitha:** “Two left.”

She sights through it.

**Tabitha:** “Don’t do anything weird.”

The player can:

- **[Stay exactly as you are.]**
- **[Put on your most painfully official expression.]**
- **[Pull a face at the last second.]**
- **“No. You first.”**

If the player lets Tabitha take the picture:

- still → **Tabitha:** “That somehow looks suspicious.”
- official → **Tabitha:** “Christ. You look electable.”
- face → **Tabitha:** “Oh, that one’s staying.”

If the player reverses the order:

**Tabitha:** “Fine.”

She gives them the camera.

**Tabitha:** “Be normal about this.”

The player directs the picture:

- **[Take it before she poses.]**
- **“Stand under the EXIT sign.”**
- **“Try to look respectable.”**

Responses:

- candid → shutter catches her halfway through objecting. **Tabitha:** “Rude.”
- EXIT → she glances up at the sign, then back at the player. **Tabitha:** “Subtle.”
- respectable → she straightens, expression becoming aggressively neutral. **Tabitha:** “There. Public-sector safe.”

## The first print

The first print slides out. Both remain at the same table while it develops.

The map now visibly contains the first print. The persistent situation text states who is in it and what happened.

The player may leave the interaction here. If they continue, the camera changes hands so the person who was the first subject becomes the second photographer.

## Second picture — reciprocal turn

### If Tabitha photographed the player first

She passes the camera over.

**Tabitha:** “Your turn. One shot. Don’t waste public resources.”

The player directs Tabitha:

- **[Take it before she poses.]**
- **“Stand under the EXIT sign.”**
- **“Try to look respectable.”**

Tabitha follows the player's direction and reacts as above.

### If the player photographed Tabitha first

She takes the camera back.

**Tabitha:** “Right. Now you.”

The player chooses their own response to being photographed:

- **[Stay exactly as you are.]**
- **[Put on your most painfully official expression.]**
- **[Pull a face at the last second.]**

Tabitha reacts according to the player's choice.

## Two prints

Both prints develop on the table.

No dialogue explains what this means for the relationship.

The player gets physical map-level choices while Tabitha remains beside them:

- **Trade prints with Tabitha.**  
  She keeps the photo of the player; the player keeps the photo of her.

- **Give Tabitha her own photo.**  
  Each keeps their own picture.

- **Leave both prints on the table.**  
  Neither person claims the moment as an object.

Tabitha's final line depends on the actual photos and the choice, not a relationship score.

Examples:

- trade after official player photo → **Tabitha:** “I’m keeping this for when you stand for parish council.”
- trade after candid Tabitha photo → **Tabitha:** “You’re not showing anyone that.”
- own photos → **Tabitha:** “Coward. Fine.”
- leave both → she looks at them once before stepping away. **Tabitha:** “Evidence destroyed by abandonment.”

## Interaction criterion

The important progression is observable without labels:

1. Tabitha starts with the camera and directs attention at the player.
2. The player can reverse the order immediately.
3. Whoever becomes photographer reacts to the subject's conduct.
4. The camera changes hands.
5. The player directly controls how Tabitha is photographed when holding it.
6. Tabitha follows that direction rather than walking off to another authored waypoint.
7. Two player-shaped physical artefacts remain.
8. The player decides how those artefacts are divided.

At no point does ordinary progress require physically chasing Tabitha.
