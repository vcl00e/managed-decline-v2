# V009 external playtest — reciprocal time succeeded; movement remains unresolved

**Date:** 2026-08-30
**Status:** positive result for the primary dyadic-interaction question; not a complete movement or presentation conclusion

## External result

The player reported that v009 **felt like spending time** with Tabitha.

This is the first recent one-to-one prototype to receive a positive answer to the central companionship question after the v007c/v007d/v008 failures.

## What this supports

V009 provides positive evidence for the following interaction pattern:

- keep both characters co-located rather than turning the NPC into successive waypoints;
- let the other character initiate, but allow the player to redirect the activity;
- give both participants roles in the same evolving activity;
- let an object/action pass between them rather than requiring the player to chase the NPC;
- use short first-pass-legible dialogue rooted in what just happened;
- leave concrete residue that records the interaction without a relationship meter.

The instant-camera premise itself is prototype material. The reusable finding is **reciprocal co-located activity**, not photography specifically.

## Presentation / baseline caveat

The player also compared the harness v003 golden control against the original narrative-interaction-lab-v003.

They reported that the recovered presentation felt **good enough**, but many details from the original v003 were absent, so the control did not feel like a complete upgrade/replacement for v003.

Therefore:

- harness v003 should be treated as a recovered **player-facing shell and regression baseline**, not a feature-complete successor to the original v003 prototype;
- original v003 remains positive evidence for richer authored detail, composition and scene texture;
- future work must not use the reduced golden control to justify dropping details that contributed to v003's appeal.

## Movement caveat

Both the harness v003 golden control and v009 can be completed without meaningful player movement.

The player explicitly noted that this may be appropriate for these particular scenarios, but should not become the default for the whole game; otherwise the presence of an explorable movement layer would lose its purpose.

This does **not** overturn the earlier v006b movement findings. It sharpens them:

> **Movement is valuable when spatial position changes participation, access, audience, witnessing, intimacy, responsibility, or what happens next. It should not be inserted merely to make the player walk.**

For intimate co-located activities, stationary or lightly mobile play can be correct. For the game as a whole, movement must still support meaningful spatial decisions such as:

- approach / remain / withdraw;
- join / stay out;
- cross a threshold;
- accompany someone as a shared decision rather than tailing them;
- intercept or miss a situation;
- choose one social situation over another by physically being elsewhere;
- move between public/private or high/low-intimacy spaces;
- discover and inhabit places whose availability changes future life.

## New design requirement

Do not infer either of these extremes:

1. `movement is unnecessary because v009 worked without it`; or
2. `every social scene must contain walking because the game has a map`.

Instead:

> **Movement must earn its place through spatial consequence. Co-located interaction must earn its place through reciprocal social consequence.**

The next movement-focused experiment should preserve the successful v009 companionship grammar while testing **shared or consequential movement**, not NPC waypoint chasing.

## Suggested next question

> **Can meaningful movement coexist with reciprocal companionship when moving changes the social terms of the situation, rather than simply transporting the player between content triggers?**

Examples worth testing later include leaving somewhere together, choosing a route or destination together, moving between public/private spaces, deciding whether to join another social situation, or one person changing course in response to the other's preference.
